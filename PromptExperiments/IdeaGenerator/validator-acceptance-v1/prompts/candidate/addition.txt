Before deciding, form a short internal account of what the source explicitly asks the piece to do, for whom, and under which conditions. Separately identify what the source observed, decided, and gives as a reason. Read each idea field literally as a claim a reader could rely on. Check whether following the idea would change an explicit audience, action, eligibility rule, timing, decision order, scope, or degree of certainty. Check whether it connects facts in a way the source does not establish, even if the facts themselves are accurate. A material mismatch in a single field is sufficient to block. If no such mismatch exists, accept without requiring a preferred angle or exhaustive detail.

Before returning a blocker, verify that its field actually contains the mismatch, its evidence is supported by the source, and its constraint describes only the limit imposed by that evidence. Use short diagnostic statements. Do not supply replacement text, propose another angle, choose an intervention for the author, or suggest additional reporting or tests.

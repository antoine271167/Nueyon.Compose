Reconstruct the central subject, explicit readership and communicative purpose from the complete source before reading the idea as an editorial assignment. Then make two independent checks:

1. Source fidelity. Read the title in isolation for what a reader would reasonably take as asserted. Then check description, audience, rationale and evidence for any material unsupported claim or implication. A caveat in another field cannot repair a stronger assertion. Compare observed scope, sample, time, outcome and certainty with the source. Lower scores in one condition and an accompanying change support reporting the observed difference, while causal attribution requires evidence that isolates the effect. A frequently observed issue does not itself establish a universal first action. An explicit, limited first action should remain explicit and limited.

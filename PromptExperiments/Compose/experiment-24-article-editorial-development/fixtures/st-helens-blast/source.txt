Evidence summary (paraphrased):
For two months before May 18, 1980, Mount St. Helens experienced earthquakes and small explosions. On May 18 its steep north flank fell in a landslide, triggering a blast that traveled nearly horizontally before a powerful vertical eruption followed. The blast developed into a gravity-driven flow of hot material that felled millions of trees, stripped bark and branches, and scraped away soil in places.

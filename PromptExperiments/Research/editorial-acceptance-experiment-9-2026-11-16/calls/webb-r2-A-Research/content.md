### Facts

1. Webb is described as the largest space telescope ever built.
2. Its fully deployed operational configuration measures approximately 70 feet long, 47 feet wide, and 28 feet tall (21 × 14 × 8 meters).
3. The fully deployed observatory was too large to fit into any available rocket.
4. The Ariane 5 rocket fairing is described as approximately 18 feet in diameter and 43 feet tall.
5. Webb was designed to fold compactly to fit inside the Ariane 5 fairing.
6. Webb’s design was driven by the mission’s science requirements and by the need to fold for launch and deploy in space during flight to L2.
7. Webb began deploying shortly after launch.
8. The solar array deployed automatically to supply power.
9. The high-gain antenna deployed automatically for high-bandwidth communications.
10. Webb engineers on the ground controlled the remaining deployments.
11. Deployment progress was monitored using telemetry data.
12. The deployment process included over 50 major deployments.
13. The process included 178 release mechanisms that had to work properly.
14. The source states that there were over 300 possible single points of failure.
15. Because Webb operates approximately one million miles from Earth, a repair mission was not possible.
16. The fore and aft sunshield pallets deployed during the first week after launch.
17. The spacecraft bus and telescope were separated by extending a telescoping tower.
18. The tower extended about two meters.
19. The sunshield membranes were unpinned and the telescoping midbooms extended, first on the port side and then on the starboard side, pulling the membranes out.
20. Tensioning the membranes was the last sunshield deployment step.
21. Radiators were also released and deployed during this period.
22. During the second week after launch, the secondary mirror tripod was unfolded and latched.
23. During the second week after launch, the two primary mirror wings were rotated and latched.
24. These steps brought Webb into its fully deployed operational configuration.
25. During the first month, the telescope and scientific instruments cooled in the shade of the sunshield; reaching stable temperatures took several weeks.
26. Strategically placed electric heater strips were used to control cooldown so that components would shrink carefully and water trapped inside the observatory could escape as gas rather than freeze as ice on mirrors or detectors.
27. The primary mirror segments and secondary mirror were unlocked, and their movement was verified.
28. Near the end of the first month, Webb performed its last mid-course maneuver to enter its optimum orbit around L2.
29. During the first month, the scientific instrument systems were powered up.
30. The remaining five months of commissioning involved aligning the optics and calibrating the scientific instruments.
31. During the second, third, and fourth months, the team began aligning the telescope optics.
32. The alignment process began by identifying which primary mirror segment corresponded to each image, moving the segments one at a time.
33. The process ended with the primary mirror segments aligned as one and the secondary mirror aligned optimally.
34. During the fifth and sixth months, the scientific instruments’ modes of operation were calibrated.
35. During commissioning, Webb demonstrated the ability to track moving targets, including nearby objects in the solar system.
36. After six months, Webb began its science mission and routine science operations.
37. The source says Webb’s deployment was completed with “remarkable smoothness.”
38. The source says the final results exceeded requirements and expectations.

### Development sequence

1. Webb was designed to fold compactly to fit inside the Ariane 5 fairing (Facts 4–6).
2. Webb launched in its folded configuration; deployments began shortly after launch (Facts 5, 7).
3. The solar array and high-gain antenna deployed automatically; ground-controlled deployments followed and were monitored through telemetry (Facts 8–11).
4. During the first week, sunshield deployment steps took place, including pallet deployment, tower extension, membrane and midboom deployment, and membrane tensioning (Facts 16–20). The source gives this order; it does not establish a relationship beyond the stated deployment sequence.
5. Radiators were also released and deployed during this period (Fact 21). Their relationship to the other listed steps is not specified.
6. During the second week, the secondary mirror tripod was unfolded and latched, and the primary mirror wings were rotated and latched; Webb then reached its fully deployed operational configuration (Facts 22–24).
7. During the first month, Webb cooled, mirror movement was verified, the last mid-course maneuver placed it into its optimum orbit around L2, and instrument systems were powered up (Facts 25–29). The source does not establish a causal relationship among these activities beyond the order and timing it describes.
8. During months two through four, telescope optics were aligned (Facts 31–33).
9. During months five and six, instrument modes were calibrated and Webb demonstrated tracking of moving targets (Facts 34–35).
10. After six months, Webb began routine science operations (Fact 36).

### Editorial relevance

- Facts 1–6 concern the folded launch design and the size and dimensions of Webb and the Ariane 5 fairing.
- Facts 12–15 concern deployment mechanisms, possible failure points, and the stated absence of a repair mission.
- Facts 7–11 and 16–24 concern deployment timing, ground control, monitoring, sunshield steps, mirror steps, and completion of the deployed configuration.
- Facts 25–35 concern cooldown, mirror checks, orbit insertion, optical alignment, and instrument calibration during commissioning.
- Facts 36–38 concern the start of routine science operations and the source’s stated assessment of deployment and final results.
- The source supports a sequence from compact launch configuration through deployment and commissioning to routine science operations. It does not explicitly state the selected idea’s broader framing that unfolding alone should not be equated with success.

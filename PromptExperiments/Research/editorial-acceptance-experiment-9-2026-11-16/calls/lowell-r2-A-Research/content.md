### Development sequence

- After 1890, total employment slipped; by 1918 textile employment was below 14,000. The source separately states that technological advances allowed output gains while mills reduced workforces.
- From World War I onward, firms began to fail or leave; Bigelow left in 1914, Middlesex ceased production in 1918, and a wave of closings followed in 1926.
- By 1936, textile employment was 8,000; many mills stood empty, housed small firms, or were partly or entirely demolished.
- During the Depression, the source records families’ hardship and workers’ irregular employment. In 1938, Merrimack workers formed a union, went on strike after demands were rejected, and returned after seven weeks when the owners capitulated.
- World War II quickened the economy and mill employment increased; after the war ended in 1945, orders fell and Lowell returned to economic doldrums.
- The Boott and Merrimack mills closed in the 1950s. In the 1960s, urban renewal affected mill properties, boardinghouses, and Little Canada; mill employment had almost disappeared. The relationship between these events and the later citizens’ initiative is not established as a direct cause.
- In the 1960s, citizens devised a redevelopment strategy and worked with planners and historians on a heritage-based plan, including a proposal for a historical park. Political and business leaders supported the movement; the city council endorsed the idea in 1972.
- Lowell Heritage State Park emerged in 1974; Lowell National Historical Park and the Lowell Historic Preservation Commission emerged in 1978.
- The University of Lowell was formed in 1975; Wang Laboratories arrived in the mid-1970s. The source identifies these among factors that contributed to Lowell’s rebirth.
- In the late 1970s and early 1980s, employment rose as business expanded, and more than 100 old buildings were rehabilitated and put to new uses.
- By the late 1980s, the boom had ended; the region’s economy cooled, companies closed or relocated, and Wang cut most of its workforce and restructured.
- In the 1992 account, the source also reports rising population and historic buildings housing new enterprises. The relationship between those observations and the earlier redevelopment measures is not specified.

### Development sequence

- Between 2013 and 2017, fees received exceeded processing costs (Fact 9).
- Late last year, a team was set up to build a refund application service and a case management application (Facts 10–11).
- The team was initially asked to deliver in a matter of weeks; it quickly discovered that this timeframe would not allow it to create a quality service for users (Facts 15–17).
- The service launched to the public in 12 weeks and passed alpha and beta Service Standard assessments (Facts 4–5). The source does not specify the precise relationship between these assessments and the launch date.
- The team focused first on refund applicants and took that work to alpha; plans for the caseworker user came later (Facts 26–30).
- During private beta, the team built the case management application while iterating the front end (Fact 31).
- During fortnightly sprints, the team used the first week for testing, research, and accepting applications, and the second week for testing, research, and processing applications while submissions were suspended (Facts 32–34).
- At the time of writing, the service had been available for five weeks, with over 100,000 applications, 92.6% reported user satisfaction, and an 80.2% reported completion rate (Facts 45–48). The source does not establish that the earlier practices caused these results.

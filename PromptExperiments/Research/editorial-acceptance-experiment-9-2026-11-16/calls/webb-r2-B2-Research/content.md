### Facts
1. Webb’s operational configuration is approximately 70 ft long, 47 ft wide and 28 ft tall, too large for an available rocket; it was designed to fold to fit the Ariane 5 fairing, approximately 18 ft in diameter and 43 ft tall. (Quick Facts)
2. Deployment involved over 50 major steps and 178 release mechanisms; the source says over 300 single points of failure could have diminished or crippled the mission. It also says no repair mission was possible at Webb’s approximately one-million-mile distance from Earth. (Quick Facts; In Depth)
3. The solar array and high-gain antenna deployed automatically. The remaining deployments were choreographed from ground control and monitored via telemetry. (Quick Facts)
4. Webb separated from the Ariane 5 upper stage after about 26 minutes of thrust; deployments began minutes after separation. On the first day, the solar array and antenna deployed, and Webb performed a trajectory-correction maneuver. (Unfolding Webb)
5. In the first week, the fore and aft sunshield pallets deployed; the spacecraft bus and telescope separated by extension of a roughly 2-meter tower; then the sunshield membranes were unpinned, drawn out by the port and starboard midbooms, and tensioned. Radiators were also released and deployed. (Unfolding Webb, “In the first week”)
6. In the second week, the secondary-mirror tripod was unfolded and latched, and both primary-mirror wings were rotated and latched. The source identifies this as bringing Webb to its fully deployed operational configuration. (Unfolding Webb, “Second Week”)
7. Deployment was followed by cooldown, instrument turn-on and insertion into orbit around L2. The source says cooling to stable temperatures took several weeks and was controlled with heater strips to manage shrinkage and allow trapped water to escape as gas rather than freeze on mirrors or detectors. Primary and secondary mirrors were unlocked and their movement verified; the last mid-course maneuver inserted Webb into its optimum L2 orbit near the end of the first month. (Unfolding Webb, “1st Month”)
8. During the second through fourth months, initial optics checks showed distorted images of the same star because the primary mirror segments were not yet aligned as one mirror. The alignment process identified which segment corresponded to each image and aligned the primary segments and secondary mirror. (Unfolding Webb, “In the second, third and fourth months”)
9. During the fifth and sixth months, the scientific instruments’ modes were calibrated while representative targets were observed; the source also reports demonstrating tracking of nearby moving targets and making Early Release Observations. (Unfolding Webb, “In the fifth and sixth months”)
10. The source says Webb began routine science operations after six months. (Unfolding Webb, “After six months”)
11. The source characterizes deployment as completed “with remarkable smoothness” and reports that cooldown met thresholds for mirror alignment and, later, for instruments to come online and be calibrated; it says final results exceeded requirements and expectations. These are source-reported evaluations. (Quick Facts)
12. The source describes design, development and intensive testing as keys to deployment success, but does not detail the tests or their individual results. (In Depth)

### Interpretations
- Webb’s transition to routine science was staged: completing mechanical deployment preceded cooldown, optical alignment and instrument calibration. (Facts 6–10)

### Unknowns
- The source does not document individual release-mechanism performance, deployment anomalies, failed checks or the results of each check. Its overall description of a smooth deployment does not establish that every mechanism or check was issue-free. (Facts 2, 11)
- It gives no specific calibration measurements, detailed acceptance thresholds, or calendar date for the start of routine science operations; it establishes only the broad commissioning sequence and that routine operations began after six months. (Facts 7–10)
- The source states that testing was important but does not provide enough detail to assess particular tests or their contribution to the outcome. (Fact 12)

### Development sequence
- Webb was folded for Ariane 5 launch (Fact 1); after separation, automatic and ground-directed deployments began (Facts 3–4). Sunshield deployment followed in the first week, then mirror structures in the second week (Facts 5–6). Cooldown, instrument turn-on and L2 insertion followed during the first month; optical checks and alignment took place in months two through four; calibration and commissioning activities continued in months five and six; routine science operations began after six months (Facts 7–10).

### Editorial relevance
- Core evidence: Facts 1–10. Reported success and its attribution limits: Facts 11–12. Key gap on whether every individual mechanism/check was trouble-free: Unknowns, first bullet.

### Facts

1. Webb is described as the largest space telescope ever built.
2. Webb’s fully deployed operational configuration measures approximately 70 feet long, 47 feet wide, and 28 feet tall.
3. The fully deployed configuration was too large to fit into any available rocket.
4. The Ariane 5 rocket fairing is approximately 18 feet in diameter and 43 feet tall.
5. Webb was designed to fold compactly to fit inside the Ariane 5 fairing.
6. Webb’s deployment began shortly after launch.
7. The solar array deployed automatically to supply Webb with power.
8. The high-gain antenna deployed for high-bandwidth communications.
9. Webb engineers on the ground orchestrated the remaining deployments.
10. Deployment progress was monitored through telemetry data.
11. The deployment process included over 50 major deployment steps.
12. The deployment process included 178 release mechanisms that had to work properly.
13. The source identifies over 300 possible single points of failure.
14. Webb operates approximately one million miles from Earth.
15. The source states that no repair mission was possible at that distance.
16. During the first week, the fore and aft sunshield pallets deployed.
17. The spacecraft bus and telescope were separated by extending a telescoping tower.
18. The telescoping tower extended about two meters.
19. The source states that the tower extension was necessary at that point for the remaining sunshield deployment to proceed.
20. The sunshield membranes were unpinned and the telescoping midbooms extended, pulling the membranes out.
21. The sunshield membranes were tensioned.
22. During the second week after launch, the secondary mirror tripod was unfolded and latched.
23. During the second week after launch, the two primary mirror wings were rotated and latched.
24. The second-week steps brought Webb into its fully deployed operational configuration.
25. The telescope and scientific instruments took several weeks to cool to stable temperatures.
26. Electric heater strips were used to control cooldown.
27. The source states that controlled cooldown was used so components would shrink carefully and water could escape as gas rather than freeze onto mirrors or detectors.
28. The primary mirror segments and secondary mirror were unlocked, and their movement was verified.
29. Near the end of the first month, Webb carried out its last mid-course maneuver to enter its optimum orbit around L2.
30. The scientific instrument systems were powered up during the first month.
31. The remaining five months of commissioning included aligning the optics and calibrating the scientific instruments.
32. In the second, third, and fourth months, Webb was pointed at a single bright star and demonstrated that it could acquire and lock onto targets.
33. Before the primary mirror segments were aligned to work as a single mirror, images of the single target star were distorted.
34. The process of aligning the telescope optics included identifying which mirror segment corresponded to each image by moving the segments one at a time.
35. In the fifth and sixth months, the scientific instruments’ modes of operation were calibrated while representative targets were observed.
36. In the fifth and sixth months, Webb demonstrated the ability to track nearby moving targets, including asteroids, comets, moons, and planets in the solar system.
37. After six months, Webb began its science mission and routine science operations.
38. The source states that Webb’s deployment was completed with “remarkable smoothness.”
39. The source states that final results exceeded requirements and expectations.

### Unknowns

- The source does not provide detailed outcomes or measurements for each alignment and calibration step.
- The source does not state that deployment completion alone meant Webb was ready for routine science operations; it describes subsequent cooldown, commissioning, alignment, and calibration before routine science operations began.
- The source does not state a general lesson about engineering or complex projects.
- The source does not provide reasons for design decisions beyond the stated need for Webb to fit inside the Ariane 5 fairing and deploy in space.

### Development sequence

1. Webb’s fully deployed configuration was too large for an available rocket; Webb was designed to fold to fit inside the Ariane 5 fairing (Facts 2–5).
2. Webb launched in its folded configuration; deployment began shortly after separation from the launch vehicle (Facts 5–6).
3. The solar array and high-gain antenna deployed automatically; ground engineers orchestrated and monitored the remaining deployments (Facts 7–10).
4. During the first week, the sunshield pallets deployed, the tower extended, and the sunshield membranes were deployed and tensioned (Facts 16–21).
5. During the second week, the secondary mirror tripod unfolded and the primary mirror wings rotated and latched; Webb reached its fully deployed operational configuration (Facts 22–24).
6. Over the following weeks, Webb cooled; mirror movement was verified and the scientific instrument systems were powered up (Facts 25–30).
7. In the second through fourth months, optical alignment took place; images were initially distorted before the primary mirror segments were aligned to work as a single mirror (Facts 32–34).
8. In the fifth and sixth months, instrument modes were calibrated and moving-target tracking was demonstrated (Facts 35–36).
9. After six months, Webb began routine science operations (Fact 37).
10. The source gives this chronology but does not establish that any one stage caused the results reported in Fact 39.

### Facts

1. The final metered record reports 410 kWh useful heat from 500 kWh electrical input over the trial boundary. A draft’s 450 kWh figure included 40 kWh duplicated during meter-file concatenation, which was removed. The ratio is for this period and boundary, not seasonal efficiency. (Performance and measurement)
2. The legacy boiler’s displayed 76% used fuel energy on a lower-heating-value basis; the electrical trial used meter input and included pump demand. No common-boundary reanalysis exists. (Performance and measurement)
3. Trial shifts processed lighter products, had ambient temperature 8 degrees higher than boiler-reference shifts, and were selected to avoid peak production. There was no random assignment or matched control. (Performance and measurement)
4. The record says it cannot isolate a storage-technology effect or quantify avoided emissions; grid-carbon intensity was not measured. The supplier’s “dependable low-carbon heat” is identified as a marketing interpretation, not a measured emissions conclusion. (Performance and measurement; dossier introduction)
5. All four vessels passed the specified pressure-hold test, but two failed the insulation surface-temperature check at the access hatch. Hot surfaces remained unresolved at sign-off, and a proposed shield was not installed. (Successful and failed controls)
6. The interlock test passed for all four vessels, but did not test the hot-surface problem. The source states that passing the pressure test does not demonstrate acceptable touch temperature. (Successful and failed controls)
7. Three thermal trips occurred during initial charging; the source defines these as protective over-temperature shutdowns, not production journeys or heat-delivery cycles. (Successful and failed controls)
8. A controller setting and sensor mounting were changed together before a repeat charge with zero trips. The record cannot separate their contributions, and states that zero trips in one repeat does not establish long-term reliability. (Successful and failed controls)
9. Three identical-input simulation runs estimated usable discharge at 402, 418 and 407 kWh. The solver used stochastic initialisation; the simulations are not additional metered field trials and do not supersede the measured 410 kWh. Solver variation limits attribution of simulation improvements. (Variability, sequencing and decision)
10. The finance appendix reports a EUR 9,200 rental allowance but gives no date or sequence relative to testing. It excludes installation, downtime and maintenance; the source supports no payback claim. (Variability, sequencing and decision)
11. Procurement approved only a longer instrumented trial conditional on a verified hatch remedy. No purchase or factory-wide roll-out was approved. (Variability, sequencing and decision)
12. Seven of twelve operators responded to a survey; five preferred the quieter charging shift. Noise was not instrumented and trial workloads differed; the source says this preference does not validate the supplier’s emissions phrase. (Variability, sequencing and decision)
13. The record reports correction of a sensor serial number in the asset register, but does not say the physical sensor was replaced. (Variability, sequencing and decision)

### Unknowns

- The source does not establish seasonal efficiency or a comparable efficiency advantage over the legacy boiler; the boundaries and energy bases differ and there is no common-boundary reanalysis (Facts 1–2).
- It does not establish that storage technology caused a performance gain, or quantify emissions avoided; shift conditions, selection, lack of controls and unmeasured grid-carbon intensity limit those conclusions (Facts 3–4).
- Acceptable touch temperature or resolution of the hatch hazard is not established: two checks failed, the issue remained unresolved at sign-off, and the proposed shield was not installed (Facts 5–6).
- The cause of the zero-trip repeat result and long-term trip reliability remain unestablished because two changes were made together and only one repeat charge is reported (Fact 8).
- The simulations do not establish additional field performance or a measured improvement; stochastic solver variation limits attribution (Fact 9).
- Payback, the rental allowance’s timing relative to tests, measured noise levels, and a physical sensor replacement are not established (Facts 10, 12–13).
- A purchase or factory-wide deployment was not approved; the source establishes only conditional approval for a longer instrumented trial (Fact 11).

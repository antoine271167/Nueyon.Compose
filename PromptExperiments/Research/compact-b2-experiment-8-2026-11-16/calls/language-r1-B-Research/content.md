### Facts
1. The source describes a fictional benefits form and states that it contains no real resident data. No general accessibility certification was commissioned. (Source preamble)
2. Reviewers sampled 80 of 640 message strings: 40 Spanish and 40 Polish. All 80 retained approved glossary entitlement terms. (Sample and linguistic checks)
3. Six sampled Spanish strings reversed the deadline-exception meaning; the Polish sample had no such reversal. A second reviewer agreed on the six reversals, while the other strings did not receive that second semantic review. (Sample and linguistic checks)
4. The source treats terminology conformity and semantic correctness as separate checks, and says neither result certifies all 640 strings. (Sample and linguistic checks)
5. An automated colour-contrast scan passed on three tested page templates. Keyboard-only testing could not activate the upload button on template two, and the defect remained open. (Access and task results)
6. On template two, screen-reader testing announced the upload label correctly but did not test keyboard activation; the source explicitly says a correct label is not evidence that a keyboard user can upload a document. (Access and task results)
7. Management approved a keyboard-fix proposal, but no deployed fix is recorded. (Access and task results)
8. The old English-only service had 33 completions in 50 moderated sessions; the multilingual form recorded 42 in 50 unmoderated sessions. The old measure required a final submitted application, while the new dashboard counted reaching the review screen. (Access and task results)
9. Participants came from different recruitment channels and were neither randomly assigned nor matched for prior experience. The source says the apparent dashboard improvement is not a comparable completion-effect estimate. (Access and task results)
10. “Case folding” normalised letter case; it did not merge benefit cases or applications. On the same 80 strings, three runs of a sampling-based checker flagged six, five and six semantic issues. Human reference review identified six deadline reversals; the checker version stayed fixed while sample order varied. (Processing and reproducibility)
11. The source says the run counts establish neither three different ground truths nor a stable checker. (Processing and reproducibility)
12. Legal approval covers only the Spanish consent paragraph; no approval date or relation to the sample review is given. Polish consent approval is not documented. Translation availability alone does not establish legal approval. (Status and decision boundary)
13. The coordinator authorised a corrected-form usability study conditional on a verified upload fix and withheld general release. The study budget includes translation fees but excludes staff time; no cost saving is demonstrated. (Status and decision boundary)
14. A satisfaction poll received 18 responses to 90 invitations; 16 respondents said wording was clearer. The source says nonresponse and self-selection prevent treating that fraction as population satisfaction; the poll did not measure successful submission or benefit uptake. (Status and decision boundary)
15. Management’s “inclusive launch” phrase is described as an ambition, not a measured inclusion outcome, and the source says it should not be repeated as a finding. (Source preamble; Status and decision boundary)
### Interpretations
- Terminology conformity, semantic correctness, and task access are distinct dimensions; a positive result on one does not establish the others. (Facts 2–6)
- The reported task figures do not support a like-for-like estimate of improved completion. (Facts 8–9)
### Unknowns
- The sampled strings do not establish the semantic correctness of all 640 strings; most sampled strings did not receive the second semantic review. (Facts 2–4)
- The scan and tests do not establish general accessibility: only three templates received the contrast scan, the upload keyboard defect was open, and no general accessibility certification was commissioned. A deployed keyboard fix is not documented. (Facts 1, 5–7)
- The differing task definitions, moderation, recruitment, and participant comparability prevent attributing the apparent difference to the multilingual form or quantifying a completion effect. Successful submission or benefit uptake was not measured by the satisfaction poll. (Facts 8–9, 14)
- The checker’s run variation does not establish its reliability or a stable issue count; the source reports one human reference review identifying six reversals. (Facts 10–11)
- The source does not establish Polish consent approval, the date or review-time relationship of Spanish consent approval, or general legal approval. (Fact 12)
- The source does not demonstrate population-level satisfaction, general inclusion, cost savings, or outcomes from the proposed corrected-form study. The study remains conditional on a verified upload fix; no general release was authorised. (Facts 13–15)
### Development sequence
- The source reports old-service sessions and multilingual-form dashboard sessions with different completion definitions and recruitment conditions; it does not establish a comparable effect or causal timeline. (Facts 8–9)
- At review, the upload defect remained open; management had approved a proposed fix, but no deployment was recorded. The coordinator authorised a usability study conditional on a verified fix and withheld general release. (Facts 5, 7, 13)

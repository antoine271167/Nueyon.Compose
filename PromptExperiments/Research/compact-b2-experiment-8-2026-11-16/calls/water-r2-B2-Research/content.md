### Facts
1. The source identifies itself as a newly constructed evaluation, not a real utility study; it covers twelve pressure loggers at two branches and is not a drinking-water safety certification. The team calls the pilot “encouraging”; this is its assessment, not an independently established result. (Source opening)
2. The final ledger records 46 low-pressure alerts in 1,400 logger-hours for the legacy fortnight and 29 in 1,120 logger-hours for the pilot fortnight. These are alerts, not confirmed leaks or affected households. A presentation’s 25 pilot alerts was corrected by restoring four suppressed, distinct device events; the source says to use 29. (Recorded outcomes)
3. The legacy threshold was 1.8 bar for 30 seconds; the pilot threshold was 1.6 bar for 60 seconds, and old traces were not reprocessed. The report does not estimate a rate-adjusted treatment effect. (Recorded outcomes)
4. The legacy fortnight alone included a planned night flushing operation. Branches were selected for ease of access, not randomly; rainfall differed, and no unaffected comparison branch was instrumented. The source says the lower alert count cannot isolate the sensor upgrade’s effect. (Recorded outcomes)
5. All twelve loggers passed static reference-gauge calibration at 2.0 bar. Separately, four failed the overnight packet-delivery check, losing 17% of scheduled messages in that check. The pressure readings that arrived were not shown to be inaccurate. (Checks and defects)
6. The packet-delivery fault remained open at review; a proposed antenna move had not been tested. Battery replacement was completed on two different devices, but no date links that work to the delivery check; the source says not to treat battery status as repair of the packet fault. (Checks and defects)
7. A bench check recorded three brownouts, defined here as transient supply-voltage drops causing device restarts, not a regional power outage. A capacitor swap and firmware update were both made before a repeat bench check with no brownouts. Their separate contributions were not identified, and the bench result does not certify field uptime. (Checks and defects)
8. Three replays of the same stored trace emitted 29, 31, and 28 alerts with a nondeterministic deduplication tie-breaker enabled. The team did not select one replay as the true count. These replay counts do not replace the final field-ledger count of 29. (Repeats and decision)
9. The source states that repeat variability and changed thresholds limit attribution. (Repeats and decision)
10. The committee authorized a six-week paired measurement study with unchanged thresholds; it did not authorize network-wide deployment. (Repeats and decision)
11. The EUR 18,000 budget covers logger rental and field visits, excludes staff overtime, and does not establish savings. No safety, leak-prevention, or annual reliability benefit was measured. Training attendance was 10 of 12 technicians, but attendance was not a competence test. (Repeats and decision)

### Interpretations
1. A successful static calibration check establishes that result for that check, but does not resolve the separately documented packet-delivery failure or establish field uptime. (Facts 5–7)
2. The observed alert-count difference is not evidence in this source of a causal sensor-upgrade effect: thresholds changed, relevant conditions differed, and the report provides no rate-adjusted treatment effect. (Facts 2–4, 9)

### Unknowns
- Whether the pressure readings received were accurate: the source says they were not shown to be inaccurate, but does not establish their accuracy. (Fact 5)
- Whether the proposed antenna move would resolve the open packet-delivery fault, or whether battery replacement affected that fault; the move was untested and no date links the replacements to the check. (Fact 6)
- Whether the capacitor swap, firmware update, or both account for the no-brownout repeat bench result, and whether that result corresponds to field uptime. (Fact 7)
- The causal or rate-adjusted effect of the sensor upgrade on alerts; the report does not estimate it, and the documented differences and confounders prevent isolating it from the raw counts. (Facts 2–4, 9)
- Whether the pilot improves safety, prevents leaks, yields annual reliability benefits, or produces savings; none was measured or established in the source. (Fact 11)
- Whether technicians were competent after training; attendance alone was not a competence test. (Fact 11)
- The outcome of the authorized six-week paired study; the source records authorization, not its completion or results. (Fact 10)

### Development sequence
- The legacy fortnight included the planned night flushing operation; the pilot fortnight used a different alert threshold. The source gives counts for both periods but does not explicitly establish their calendar order. (Facts 2–4)
- The capacitor swap and firmware update preceded the repeat bench check with no brownouts. (Fact 7)
- The committee authorized a future six-week paired study; network-wide deployment was not authorized. (Fact 10)

### Editorial relevance
- Core evidence: Facts 2–9; Interpretations 1–2.
- Decision limits and unmeasured outcomes: Facts 10–11; Unknowns.

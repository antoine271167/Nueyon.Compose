### Facts

1. The note describes a fictional benefits form and contains no real resident data. Management’s “inclusive launch” phrase states an ambition, not a measured inclusion outcome; no general accessibility certification was commissioned. (Opening)
2. Reviewers sampled 80 of 640 message strings: 40 Spanish and 40 Polish. All 80 retained approved glossary entitlement terms, but this does not certify the remaining strings. (Sample and linguistic checks)
3. Six Spanish strings reversed the deadline exception’s meaning; the Polish sample had no such reversal. Terminology conformity and semantic correctness were separate checks. A second reviewer agreed on the six reversals; the remaining strings did not receive that second semantic review. (Sample and linguistic checks)
4. An automated contrast scan passed on three tested page templates. Keyboard-only testing could not activate the document-upload button on the second template, and that defect was still open. Screen-reader testing announced the label correctly there but did not test keyboard activation. Management approved a keyboard-fix proposal; no deployed fix is recorded. (Access and task results)
5. The old English-only service completed 33 of 50 moderated sessions, with completion requiring a final submitted application. The multilingual form recorded 42 of 50 unmoderated sessions, counting reaching the review screen. Participants came from different recruitment channels and were neither randomly assigned nor matched for prior experience. The source says this is not a comparable completion-effect estimate. (Access and task results)
6. “Case folding” means normalising letter case; it did not merge benefit cases or applications. (Processing and reproducibility)
7. On the same 80 strings, three checker runs flagged six, five, and six semantic issues. The checker version was fixed, but sample order varied. A human reference review identified the six deadline reversals. The automated counts do not establish three different ground truths or a stable checker. (Processing and reproducibility)
8. Legal approval covers only the Spanish consent paragraph. No approval date or timing relative to the sample review is given; Polish consent approval is not documented. Translation availability alone does not establish legal approval. (Status and decision boundary)
9. The coordinator authorised a corrected-form usability study conditional on a verified upload fix and withheld general release. (Status and decision boundary)
10. The study budget includes translation fees but excludes staff time; no cost saving is demonstrated. (Status and decision boundary)
11. A satisfaction poll received 18 responses to 90 invitations; 16 respondents said wording was clearer. The note says nonresponse and self-selection prevent treating this fraction as population satisfaction. The poll did not measure successful submission or benefits uptake. (Status and decision boundary)

### Unknowns

- Whether unreviewed strings contain semantic errors; the second semantic review covered only the six identified reversals (F3).
- Accessibility beyond the specific checks reported, and whether keyboard upload works after a fix; the defect was open and no deployed fix is recorded (F1, F4).
- A comparable completion effect for the multilingual form: the measures, recruitment, and participant comparability differ (F5).
- Whether the checker’s varying counts reflect sampling variation or other causes; the source does not establish checker stability or separate ground truths (F7).
- The date of Spanish consent approval and its timing relative to the sample review, as well as whether Polish consent received approval (F8).
- The corrected-form study’s results, population-level satisfaction, successful submission and benefits uptake, and any cost saving are not established by the reported evidence (F9–F11).

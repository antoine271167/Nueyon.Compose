### Facts

1. The final metered record reports 410 kWh useful heat from 500 kWh electrical input over the trial boundary. A draft’s 450 kWh was corrected because 40 kWh had been duplicated during meter-file concatenation. The ratio is for this period and boundary, not seasonal efficiency. (Performance and measurement)
2. The legacy boiler’s displayed 76% used fuel energy on a lower-heating-value basis; the electrical trial used meter input and included pump demand. The source says no common-boundary reanalysis exists. (Performance and measurement)
3. Trial shifts processed lighter products, had ambient temperature 8 degrees higher than the boiler-reference shifts, and were selected to avoid peak production. There was no random assignment or matched control; the record cannot isolate a storage-technology effect or quantify avoided emissions. Grid-carbon intensity was not measured. (Performance and measurement)
4. All four vessels passed the specified pressure-hold test. Two failed the insulation surface-temperature check at the access hatch; hot surfaces remained unresolved at sign-off, and a proposed shield was not installed. The source explicitly says pressure-test success does not demonstrate acceptable touch temperature. (Successful and failed controls)
5. The interlock test passed for all four vessels, but did not test the hot-surface problem. (Successful and failed controls)
6. Three thermal trips occurred during initial charging. The source defines a thermal trip as a protective over-temperature shutdown, not a production journey or heat-delivery cycle. (Successful and failed controls)
7. A controller setting and sensor mounting were changed together before a repeat charge, which had zero trips. The record cannot separate the actions’ contributions, and says zero trips in one repeat does not establish long-term reliability. (Successful and failed controls)
8. Three identical-input simulation runs estimated usable discharge at 402, 418 and 407 kWh; the solver used stochastic initialisation. These were not additional metered field trials and do not supersede the measured 410 kWh. The source identifies solver variation as an attribution limit for interpreting simulation improvements. (Variability, sequencing and decision)
9. The finance appendix reports a EUR 9,200 rental allowance, but gives no date or sequence relative to testing. It excludes installation, downtime and maintenance; the source says no payback claim is supported. (Variability, sequencing and decision)
10. Procurement approved only a longer instrumented trial, conditional on a verified hatch remedy. No purchase or factory-wide roll-out was approved. (Variability, sequencing and decision)
11. Seven of twelve operators responded to a survey; five preferred the quieter charging shift. Noise was not instrumented and trial workloads differed. The source says this preference does not validate the supplier’s “dependable low-carbon heat” phrase. (Variability, sequencing and decision; opening note)
12. The record says a sensor serial number was corrected in the asset register; it does not say the physical sensor was replaced. (Variability, sequencing and decision)
13. The memorandum describes itself as a fictional engineering dossier, not a safety approval or annual energy model, and calls “dependable low-carbon heat” a supplier marketing interpretation rather than a measured emissions conclusion. (Opening note)

### Unknowns

- Seasonal performance and a valid like-for-like efficiency comparison remain unestablished: the source provides no seasonal model or common-boundary reanalysis. (Facts 1–3)
- The storage technology’s independent effect, avoided emissions, and any emissions benefit are not established by this trial; grid-carbon intensity was not measured. (Fact 3)
- The source does not report a completed hatch remedy or acceptable touch temperatures at sign-off; the proposed shield was not installed. (Fact 4)
- The individual contributions of the controller-setting and sensor-mounting changes, and long-term trip reliability, are not established. (Fact 7)
- The simulations do not establish additional field-trial performance; their run-to-run differences cannot be attributed to a technology improvement from this record. (Fact 8)
- The rental allowance does not establish payback, and its timing relative to testing is unspecified. (Fact 9)
- Instrumented noise levels and a workload-controlled basis for the survey preference are absent. (Fact 11)
- Physical sensor replacement is not established by the asset-register correction. (Fact 12)

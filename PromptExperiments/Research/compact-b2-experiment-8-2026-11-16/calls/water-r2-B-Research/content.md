### Facts
1. The dossier is a synthetic evaluation, not a real utility study; it covers 12 pressure loggers at two branches and is not a drinking-water safety certification. The team calls the pilot “encouraging”; the source identifies this as the team’s assessment, not an independently established result. (“Larch water telemetry review”)
2. All 12 loggers passed a static reference-gauge calibration at 2.0 bar. (“Checks and defects”)
3. Four loggers failed the overnight packet-delivery check, losing 17% of scheduled messages in that check. The pressure readings that arrived were not shown to be inaccurate. (“Checks and defects”)
4. The packet-delivery fault remained open; a proposed antenna move had not been tested. Battery replacement was completed on two different devices, but no date links that work to the delivery check; the source says not to treat it as repair of the packet fault. (“Checks and defects”)
5. A bench check recorded three brownouts, defined as transient supply-voltage drops causing device restarts, not a regional power outage. (“Checks and defects”)
6. A capacitor swap and firmware update preceded a repeat bench check with no brownouts. Their separate contributions were not identified, and the bench result does not certify field uptime. (“Checks and defects”)
7. The final ledger records 46 low-pressure alerts in 1,400 logger-hours for the legacy fortnight and 29 in 1,120 logger-hours for the pilot fortnight. These are alerts, not confirmed leaks or affected households. A presentation had reported 25 pilot alerts; the final ledger restored four suppressed duplicates that were distinct device events, and the source specifies using 29. (“Recorded outcomes”)
8. The legacy threshold was 1.8 bar for 30 seconds; the pilot threshold was 1.6 bar for 60 seconds. Old traces were not reprocessed. (“Recorded outcomes”)
9. Night flushing occurred during the legacy fortnight only. Branches were selected for ease of access, not randomly; rainfall differed, and no unaffected comparison branch was instrumented. The source says the lower alert count cannot isolate the sensor upgrade’s effect. (“Recorded outcomes”)
10. The report does not estimate a rate-adjusted treatment effect. (“Recorded outcomes”)
11. Three replays of the same stored trace emitted 29, 31 and 28 alerts, with a nondeterministic deduplication tie-breaker enabled. The team did not select a replay as the true count; replay counts do not replace the final field ledger of 29. The source says repeat variability and changed thresholds limit attribution. (“Repeats and decision”)
12. The committee authorized a six-week paired measurement study with unchanged thresholds, but did not authorize network-wide deployment. (“Repeats and decision”)
13. The EUR 18,000 budget covers logger rental and field visits, excludes staff overtime, and does not establish savings. No safety, leak-prevention or annual reliability benefit was measured. (“Repeats and decision”)

### Interpretations
- The successful static calibration establishes that specific check result, not a clean bill of operational reliability; packet delivery and field uptime have separate documented limitations (Facts 2–6).
- The two raw alert totals do not establish that the sensor upgrade caused a reduction (Facts 7–11).

### Unknowns
- The source does not establish the upgrade’s causal effect on alerts: thresholds changed, legacy traces were not reprocessed, other conditions differed, there was no unaffected comparison branch, and no rate-adjusted treatment effect was estimated (Facts 8–10).
- It is not established whether the packet fault was later corrected, whether the proposed antenna move would help, or whether the battery replacements related to that fault. Accuracy of readings that arrived is not established beyond the source’s statement that they were not shown to be inaccurate (Facts 3–4).
- The separate effects of the capacitor swap and firmware update, and field uptime after the bench changes, are not established (Fact 6).
- The source reports no measured safety, leak-prevention or annual reliability benefit, and no network-wide deployment authorization; it provides no outcome from the authorized six-week study (Facts 12–13).

### Development sequence
- The legacy fortnight recorded 46 alerts; the pilot fortnight recorded 29 in the final ledger, under changed thresholds and differing conditions (Facts 7–10).
- The bench check recorded three brownouts; the capacitor swap and firmware update occurred before a repeat bench check with no brownouts (Facts 5–6).
- After reviewing the pilot, the committee authorized a six-week paired study with unchanged thresholds, not network-wide deployment (Fact 12).

### Editorial relevance
Facts 2–6 establish the distinction between calibration, delivery faults and limited bench evidence; Facts 7–11 establish the alert comparison and attribution limits; Facts 12–13 establish the decision and measured-benefit limits. Interpretations 1–2 summarize those distinctions.

### Facts
1. The source identifies itself as a newly constructed, non-real utility evaluation; it covers 12 pressure loggers at two branches and is not a drinking-water safety certification. The team calls the pilot “encouraging”; this is the team’s assessment, not an independently established result. (Opening)
2. The legacy fortnight recorded 46 low-pressure alerts in 1,400 logger-hours; the pilot fortnight recorded 29 in 1,120 logger-hours. These are alert counts, not confirmed leaks or affected households. The final ledger uses 29 after restoring four suppressed duplicates that were distinct device events; a presentation’s 25 is not the final count. The report does not estimate a rate-adjusted treatment effect. (“Recorded outcomes”)
3. The legacy threshold was 1.8 bar for 30 seconds; the pilot threshold was 1.6 bar for 60 seconds, and old traces were not reprocessed. Night flushing occurred during the legacy fortnight only; branches were selected for ease of access, not randomly; rainfall differed; and no unaffected comparison branch was instrumented. The source says the lower alert count cannot isolate the sensor upgrade’s effect. (“Recorded outcomes”)
4. All 12 loggers passed the static reference-gauge calibration at 2.0 bar. Separately, four failed the overnight packet-delivery check, losing 17% of scheduled messages in that check. The source does not show that the pressure readings which arrived were inaccurate. (“Checks and defects”)
5. The packet-delivery fault remained open at review. A proposed antenna move had not been tested. Battery replacement was completed on two different devices, but no date links that work to the delivery check; the source says not to treat battery status as a repair of the packet fault. (“Checks and defects”)
6. A bench check recorded three brownouts, defined here as transient supply-voltage drops causing device restarts, not a regional power outage. A capacitor swap and firmware update were both made before a repeat bench check with no brownouts. Their separate contributions were not identified, and the bench result does not certify field uptime. (“Checks and defects”)
7. Three replays of the same stored trace emitted 29, 31 and 28 alerts; a nondeterministic deduplication tie-breaker was enabled. The team did not select a replay as the true count. These replay counts do not replace the final field ledger of 29; the source says repeat variability and changed thresholds limit attribution. (“Repeats and decision”)
8. The committee authorized a six-week paired measurement study with unchanged thresholds, but did not authorize network-wide deployment. The EUR 18,000 budget covers logger rental and field visits, excludes staff overtime, and does not establish savings. No safety, leak-prevention or annual reliability benefit was measured. Training attendance was 10 of 12 technicians, but attendance was not a competence test. (“Repeats and decision”)
### Interpretations
1. Passing the static calibration establishes that check’s result only; it does not resolve the separately documented delivery fault or establish field uptime. (Facts 4–6)
2. The recorded alert-count comparison is not an isolated estimate of the sensor upgrade’s effect. (Facts 2–3, 7)
### Unknowns
- The source does not establish whether the sensor upgrade caused a reduction in alerts, nor provide a rate-adjusted treatment effect; thresholds, flushing, branch selection, rainfall, lack of a comparison branch, and replay variability constrain attribution. (Facts 2–3, 7)
- It does not establish the accuracy of pressure readings beyond noting that the readings received were not shown to be inaccurate; the packet-loss check also means scheduled messages were missing. (Facts 4–5)
- The cause or repair of the open packet-delivery fault is unresolved; the proposed antenna move was untested, and the battery work is not linked to or established as a repair of that fault. (Fact 5)
- The separate effects of the capacitor swap and firmware update, and field uptime after those changes, are not established by the repeat bench check. (Fact 6)
- The authorized paired study has not been reported as completed here; no network-wide deployment decision, savings, safety benefit, leak-prevention benefit, annual reliability benefit, or technician competence result is established. (Fact 8)
### Development sequence
- The source records legacy and pilot fortnight outcomes, without supplying dates that establish their calendar sequence. (Facts 2–3)
- The capacitor swap and firmware update preceded a repeat bench check that recorded no brownouts; their individual effects and field performance remain unresolved. (Fact 6)
- At review, the packet fault remained open and the antenna move was still proposed and untested. The committee authorized a six-week paired measurement study, but not network-wide deployment. (Facts 5, 8)
### Editorial relevance
Facts 2–3 and 7: observed counts and attribution limits; Facts 4–6: distinct checks, faults and technical qualifications; Fact 8: decision boundary and unmeasured outcomes. Interpretations 1–2 summarize these distinctions.

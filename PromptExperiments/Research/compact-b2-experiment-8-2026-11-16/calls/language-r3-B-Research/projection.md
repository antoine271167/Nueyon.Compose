### Facts

1. The source describes a fictional benefits form and contains no real resident data. Management’s “inclusive launch” is stated as an ambition, not a measured inclusion outcome; no general accessibility certification was commissioned. (“synthetic audit note”)
2. Reviewers sampled 80 of 640 message strings: 40 Spanish and 40 Polish. All 80 retained approved glossary entitlement terms, but six Spanish strings reversed the deadline exception; none of the Polish sample did. Terminology conformity and semantic correctness were separate checks. (“Sample and linguistic checks”)
3. A second reviewer agreed with the six Spanish reversals. The remaining strings did not receive that second semantic review; the sample results do not certify all 640 strings. (“Sample and linguistic checks”)
4. Automated colour-contrast testing passed three page templates. Keyboard-only testing could not activate the document-upload button on template two, and the defect remained open. Screen-reader testing correctly announced its label but did not test keyboard activation. (“Access and task results”)
5. Management approved a keyboard-fix proposal, but no deployed fix is recorded. The source explicitly cautions that a visually correct label does not establish that keyboard users can upload a document. (“Access and task results”)
6. The old English-only service recorded completion in 33 of 50 moderated sessions; the multilingual form recorded 42 of 50 unmoderated sessions. The old measure required a final submitted application, while the new dashboard counted reaching the review screen. (“Access and task results”)
7. Participants came through different recruitment channels and were neither randomly assigned nor matched for prior experience. The source says the apparent dashboard improvement is not a comparable completion-effect estimate. (“Access and task results”)
8. “Case folding” normalised letter case; it did not merge benefit cases or applications. On the same 80 strings, three runs of a sampling-based checker flagged six, five and six semantic issues; the checker version was fixed, but sample order varied. (“Processing and reproducibility”)
9. A human reference review identified six deadline reversals. The source says the automated counts establish neither three different ground truths nor a stable checker. (“Processing and reproducibility”)
10. Legal approval covers only the Spanish consent paragraph. No approval date or temporal relationship to the sample review is given, and Polish consent approval is not documented; translation availability alone does not establish legal approval. (“Status and decision boundary”)
11. The coordinator authorised a corrected-form usability study conditional on a verified upload fix, and withheld general release. The study budget includes translation fees but excludes staff time; no cost saving is demonstrated. (“Status and decision boundary”)
12. A separate satisfaction poll had 18 responses from 90 invitations; 16 respondents said wording was clearer. The source identifies nonresponse and self-selection as barriers to treating that fraction as population satisfaction. The poll did not measure successful submission or benefit uptake. (“Status and decision boundary”)

### Unknowns

- The source does not establish semantic correctness across all 640 strings, or independent second-review confirmation beyond the six identified reversals (Facts 2–3).
- It does not establish keyboard upload access on template two after a fix: the defect was open, and no deployed fix or post-fix verification is recorded (Facts 4–5, 11).
- It does not establish overall accessibility from the three-template contrast scan, correct screen-reader label, or any accessibility certification (Facts 1, 4–5).
- It does not establish that the multilingual form caused higher completion, or provide a comparable estimate of final application submission (Facts 6–7).
- It does not establish checker stability or a single automated ground truth from the three run counts (Facts 8–9).
- It does not establish Polish consent approval, when Spanish approval occurred, or whether that approval preceded the sample review (Fact 10).
- It does not establish population-level satisfaction, successful submission, benefit uptake, or cost savings (Facts 11–12).

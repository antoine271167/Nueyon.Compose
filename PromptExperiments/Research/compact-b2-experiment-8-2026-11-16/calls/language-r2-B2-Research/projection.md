### Facts

1. The source describes a fictional benefits form, contains no real resident data, and says no general accessibility certification was commissioned. It says management’s “inclusive launch” phrase is an ambition, not a measured inclusion outcome. (Source note)
2. Reviewers sampled 80 of 640 message strings: 40 Spanish and 40 Polish. All 80 retained approved glossary entitlement terms, but six Spanish strings reversed the deadline exception’s meaning; no such reversal was found in the Polish sample. Terminology conformity and semantic correctness were separate checks, and neither result certifies all 640 strings. (Sample and linguistic checks)
3. A second reviewer agreed with the six identified reversals; the other strings did not receive that second semantic review. (Sample and linguistic checks)
4. An automated colour-contrast scan passed on the three tested page templates. Keyboard-only testing could not activate the document-upload button on the second template, and the defect was still open. (Access and task results)
5. On that template, screen-reader testing announced the upload label correctly but did not test keyboard activation. The source explicitly says a visually correct label does not show that a keyboard user can upload a document. Management approved a keyboard-fix proposal; no deployed fix is recorded. (Access and task results)
6. The old English-only service recorded completion in 33 of 50 moderated sessions; the multilingual form recorded 42 of 50 unmoderated sessions. The old measure required a final submitted application, while the new dashboard counted reaching the review screen. Participants came from different recruitment channels and were neither randomly assigned nor matched for prior experience. The source says the apparent dashboard improvement is not a comparable completion-effect estimate. (Access and task results)
7. “Case folding” means normalising letter case in preprocessing; it did not merge benefit cases or applications. On the same 80 strings, a sampling-based checker flagged six, five and six semantic issues across three runs. A human reference review identified six deadline reversals. The checker version was fixed, but sample order varied; the run counts do not establish three different ground truths or a stable checker. (Processing and reproducibility)
8. The appendix says legal approval covers only the Spanish consent paragraph. It gives no approval date or temporal relationship to the sample review; Polish consent approval is not documented. The source states that translation availability alone does not establish legal approval. (Status and decision boundary)
9. The coordinator authorised a corrected-form usability study conditional on a verified upload fix and withheld general release. The study budget includes translation fees but excludes staff time; no cost saving is demonstrated. (Status and decision boundary)
10. A satisfaction poll received 18 responses from 90 invitations; 16 respondents said wording was clearer. The source says nonresponse and self-selection prevent treating this fraction as population satisfaction. The poll did not measure successful submission or benefit uptake. (Status and decision boundary)

### Unknowns

- The semantic correctness of all 640 strings is not established: only 80 were sampled, and the remaining strings did not receive the second semantic review. The source does not establish whether unsampled strings contain errors. (Facts 2–3)
- Keyboard operation beyond the tested failure is not established, and the approved fix proposal’s implementation or verification is not recorded. A contrast pass on three templates and correct screen-reader announcement of one label do not establish overall accessibility. (Facts 1, 4–5, 9)
- The source does not support a comparable completion-effect estimate or establish population-level satisfaction, successful submission, or benefit uptake from the cited poll and session results. (Facts 6, 10)
- The three checker counts do not establish checker stability or separate ground truths; the source does not report a verified general-release outcome, a deployed upload fix, or demonstrated cost savings. (Facts 7, 9)
- Legal approval beyond the Spanish consent paragraph—including Polish consent approval—is not established; the approval’s date and relation to the sample review are unspecified. (Fact 8)

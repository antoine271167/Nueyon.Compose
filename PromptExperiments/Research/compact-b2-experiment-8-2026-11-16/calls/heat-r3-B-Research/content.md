### Facts
1. The final metered record reports 410 kWh useful heat from 500 kWh electrical input for the trial boundary. A draft’s 450 kWh figure included 40 kWh duplicated during meter-file concatenation, which was removed. (“Performance and measurement”)
2. The 410/500 ratio applies only to this trial boundary and period, not seasonal efficiency. The legacy boiler’s displayed 76% used fuel energy on a lower-heating-value basis; the electrical trial used meter input and included pump demand. No common-boundary reanalysis exists. (“not seasonal efficiency”; “No common-boundary reanalysis”)
3. Trial shifts processed lighter products, had ambient temperatures 8 degrees higher than boiler-reference shifts, and were selected to avoid peak production. There was no random assignment or matched control; the record says it cannot isolate a storage-technology effect or quantify avoided emissions, and grid-carbon intensity was not measured. (“Performance and measurement”)
4. All four vessels passed the specified pressure-hold test, but two failed the insulation surface-temperature check at the access hatch. Hot surfaces remained unresolved at sign-off, and a proposed shield was not installed. The source states that passing the pressure test does not demonstrate acceptable touch temperature. (“Successful and failed controls”)
5. The interlock test passed on all four vessels but did not test the hot-surface problem. (“Successful and failed controls”)
6. Three thermal trips—defined as protective over-temperature shutdowns, not production journeys or heat-delivery cycles—occurred during initial charging. Controller setting and sensor mounting were changed together before a repeat charge with zero trips; the record cannot separate their contributions, and one zero-trip repeat does not establish long-term reliability. (“Successful and failed controls”)
7. Three identical-input simulation runs estimated usable discharge at 402, 418 and 407 kWh. They were stochastic simulations, not additional metered field trials, and do not supersede the measured 410 kWh; solver variation limits attribution of simulation improvements. (“Variability, sequencing and decision”)
8. The finance appendix reports a EUR 9,200 rental allowance but gives no date or sequence relative to testing. It excludes installation, downtime and maintenance, so the source supports no payback claim. (“The finance appendix”; “no payback claim is supported”)
9. Procurement approved only a longer instrumented trial conditional on a verified hatch remedy; it approved neither purchase nor factory-wide roll-out. (“Variability, sequencing and decision”)
10. The supplier’s phrase “dependable low-carbon heat” is identified as a marketing interpretation, not a measured emissions conclusion. (“opening note”; “Performance and measurement”)

### Interpretations
1. The record shows a positive measured heat-output result alongside unresolved control and comparison limits (Facts 1–6); it does not establish broad system quality or comparative efficiency.
2. The procurement decision is conditional further testing, not deployment approval (Fact 9).

### Unknowns
- Seasonal efficiency and a valid efficiency comparison with the legacy boiler are not established; the source provides no common-boundary reanalysis (Fact 2).
- The extent to which storage technology caused the measured result, or how much energy or emissions it avoided, is not established because of the shift differences and absent matched control, random assignment and grid-carbon measurement (Fact 3).
- Whether the hatch hot-surface issue was remedied after sign-off is not established; the proposed shield was not installed and the remedy is a condition of the longer trial (Facts 4, 9).
- The separate contributions of the controller change and sensor-mounting change to the zero-trip repeat, and longer-term trip reliability, are not established (Fact 6).
- The simulations do not establish additional field performance or an improvement attributable to a change (Fact 7).
- Payback, purchase, factory-wide deployment, and a measured low-carbon outcome are not established (Facts 8–10).

### Development sequence
- Initial charging produced three protective over-temperature shutdowns; the controller setting and sensor mounting were then changed together, followed by one repeat charge with zero trips (Fact 6).
- At sign-off, two vessels still failed the hatch surface-temperature check and the proposed shield had not been installed (Fact 4). Procurement subsequently approved a longer instrumented trial conditional on a verified hatch remedy, not purchase or roll-out (Fact 9).
- Separately, the draft 450 kWh figure was corrected by removing 40 kWh of duplicated data, yielding the final 410 kWh metered record (Fact 1).

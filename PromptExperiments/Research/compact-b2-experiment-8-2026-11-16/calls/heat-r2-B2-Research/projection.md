### Facts

1. The dossier describes a two-shift factory trial and states it is “not a safety approval or annual energy model.” (Opening)
2. The final metered record reports 410 kWh useful heat from 500 kWh electrical input. A draft’s 450 kWh useful-heat figure included 40 kWh duplicated during meter-file concatenation, which was removed. (Performance and measurement)
3. The 410/500 ratio applies only to the trial boundary and period. The legacy boiler’s displayed 76% used fuel energy on a lower-heating-value basis; the electrical trial used meter input and included pump demand. The source says there is no common-boundary reanalysis. (Performance and measurement)
4. Trial shifts processed lighter products, had ambient temperature 8 degrees higher than the boiler-reference shifts, and were selected by operators to avoid peak production. There was no random assignment or matched control. The record says it cannot isolate a storage-technology effect or quantify avoided emissions; grid-carbon intensity was not measured. (Performance and measurement)
5. All four vessels passed the specified pressure-hold test, but two failed the insulation surface-temperature check at the access hatch. Hot surfaces remained unresolved at sign-off, and a proposed shield was not installed. The source explicitly says the pressure test does not demonstrate acceptable touch temperature. (Successful and failed controls)
6. The interlock test passed for all four vessels, but did not test the hot-surface problem. (Successful and failed controls)
7. Three thermal trips occurred during initial charging. The source defines a thermal trip as a protective over-temperature shutdown, not a production journey or heat-delivery cycle. A controller setting and sensor mounting were changed together before a repeat charge, which had zero trips; the record cannot separate their contributions and says one repeat does not establish long-term reliability. (Successful and failed controls)
8. Three identical-input simulation runs estimated usable discharge at 402, 418 and 407 kWh; the solver used stochastic initialisation. The source says these are not additional metered field trials and do not supersede the measured 410 kWh, and identifies solver variation as an attribution limit for interpreting simulation improvements. (Variability, sequencing and decision)
9. The finance appendix reports a EUR 9,200 rental allowance but gives no date or sequence relative to testing. It excludes installation, downtime and maintenance; the source says no payback claim is supported. (Variability, sequencing and decision)
10. Procurement approved only a longer instrumented trial conditional on a verified hatch remedy; no purchase or factory-wide roll-out was approved. (Variability, sequencing and decision)
11. Seven of twelve operators responded to the survey, and five preferred the quieter charging shift. Noise was not instrumented and trial workloads differed. The supplier’s phrase “dependable low-carbon heat” is described as marketing, not a measured emissions conclusion, and the source says the preference does not validate it. (Opening; Variability, sequencing and decision)
12. The record says a sensor serial number was corrected in the asset register; it does not say the physical sensor was replaced. (Variability, sequencing and decision)

### Unknowns

- Seasonal efficiency and a directly comparable boiler-versus-store efficiency result are not established; the reported ratio is limited to the trial boundary and the source reports no common-boundary reanalysis (Facts 2–3).
- The storage technology’s causal contribution to the observed trial result, and any quantified avoided emissions, remain unestablished under the reported shift differences and measurement gaps (Fact 4).
- Long-term reliability, and the separate contributions of the controller-setting and sensor-mounting changes to the zero-trip repeat, are not established (Fact 7).
- The simulations do not establish additional metered field performance; the record does not establish an improvement attributable to a particular change (Fact 8).
- Payback is unsupported by the stated allowance, which excludes costs and has no stated timing relative to testing (Fact 9).
- Whether the hatch hot-surface issue was remedied after sign-off, or whether the physical sensor was replaced, is not established (Facts 5, 12).
- Instrumented noise levels and a measured emissions conclusion are absent; operator preference does not establish either (Fact 11).

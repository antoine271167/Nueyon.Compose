### Facts

1. The dossier is a newly constructed evaluation source, not a real utility study.
2. The review concerns twelve pressure loggers at two branches.
3. The review is not a drinking-water safety certification.
4. The team calls the pilot encouraging.
5. The source describes that assessment as the team’s assessment, not an independently established result.
6. The legacy fortnight recorded 46 low-pressure alerts in 1,400 logger-hours.
7. The pilot fortnight recorded 29 low-pressure alerts in 1,120 logger-hours.
8. The counts are alerts, not confirmed leaks or affected households.
9. A presentation reported 25 pilot alerts.
10. The final ledger restored four suppressed duplicates whose timestamps were distinct device events.
11. The report does not estimate a rate-adjusted treatment effect.
12. The legacy alert threshold was 1.8 bar for 30 seconds.
13. The pilot alert threshold was 1.6 bar for 60 seconds.
14. Old traces were not reprocessed.
15. A planned night flushing operation occurred during the legacy fortnight only.
16. Branches were selected for ease of access, not randomly.
17. Rainfall differed between the fortnights.
18. No unaffected comparison branch was instrumented.
19. The source states that the lower alert count cannot isolate the sensor upgrade’s effect.
20. All twelve loggers passed the static reference-gauge calibration at 2.0 bar.
21. Four loggers failed the overnight packet-delivery check.
22. Those four loggers lost 17% of scheduled messages in that check.
23. The pressure readings that arrived were not shown to be inaccurate.
24. The delivery fault remained open at review.
25. A proposed antenna move had not been tested.
26. Battery replacement was completed on two different devices.
27. The dossier provides no date linking the battery replacement work to the delivery check.
28. A bench check recorded three brownouts.
29. The source defines the brownouts as transient supply-voltage drops causing device restarts, not a regional power outage.
30. A capacitor swap and a firmware update were both made before a repeat bench check.
31. The repeat bench check recorded no brownouts.
32. The separate contributions of the capacitor swap and firmware update were not identified.
33. The bench result does not certify field uptime.
34. Three replays of the same stored trace emitted 29, 31, and 28 alerts.
35. A nondeterministic deduplication tie-breaker was enabled.
36. The team did not select one replay as the true count.
37. The replay counts do not replace the final field ledger count of 29.
38. The source states that repeat variability and changed thresholds limit attribution.
39. The committee authorized a six-week paired measurement study with unchanged thresholds.
40. The committee did not authorize network-wide deployment.
41. The EUR 18,000 budget covers logger rental and field visits.
42. The budget excludes staff overtime.
43. The budget does not establish savings.
44. No safety, leak-prevention, or annual reliability benefit was measured.
45. Training attendance was 10 of 12 technicians.
46. Attendance was not a competence test.

### Unknowns

- Whether passing the static reference-gauge calibration at 2.0 bar establishes reliability under other conditions or overall field reliability; the source reports the calibration result but does not establish either conclusion.
- Whether the sensor upgrade caused any portion of the observed alert-count difference. The source says the lower count cannot isolate its effect.
- A rate-adjusted treatment effect; the report does not estimate one.
- Whether the pressure readings in messages that were not delivered would have been accurate.
- Whether the proposed antenna move would address the packet-delivery fault; it had not been tested.
- Whether the battery replacements were related to, or repaired, the packet-delivery fault; the dossier gives no date linking the work to the check.
- The separate contributions of the capacitor swap and firmware update to the repeat bench result.
- Whether the absence of brownouts in the repeat bench check corresponds to field uptime; the source says the bench result does not certify field uptime.
- Which replay count, if any, should be treated as the true replay count; the team did not select one.
- The cause of the differences between the replay counts.
- Whether the authorized six-week study will support network-wide deployment or establish measured benefits; the source reports the authorization, not its results.
- Whether the pilot produced savings or safety, leak-prevention, or annual reliability benefits; none was measured in this dossier.
- Whether technician attendance indicates competence; attendance was not a competence test.
- The source does not establish that the pilot was reliable overall, nor does it establish that it was unreliable overall.

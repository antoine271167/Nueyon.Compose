### Facts

1. The supplier reviewed three shipments.
2. The supplier used dimensional tolerance and surface finish as two independent acceptance dimensions.
3. Passing one dimension does not imply passing the other.
4. Shipment Larch met tolerance on all 40 inspected components.
5. Six inspected components from Shipment Larch had surface scratches.
6. Shipment Larch failed finish acceptance.
7. Shipment Birch had no scratches among its 40 inspected components.
8. Four inspected components from Shipment Birch were outside tolerance.
9. Shipment Birch failed dimensional acceptance.
10. Shipment Hazel passed both dimensions on all 40 inspected components.
11. Inspectors selected accessible top-layer components rather than a random sample of the shipments.
12. No counts for uninspected lower layers are available.
13. The supplier replaced packing foam after Shipment Larch.
14. Shipment Birch also used a different machining batch.
15. No isolated foam-effect test was done.
16. The report recommends a randomized deeper inspection before changing the acceptance rule.
17. The recommendation has not been tested.
18. The report does not recommend dropping either dimension.
19. A separate label-printing delay affected Shipment Hazel’s delivery timing.
20. The label-printing delay did not affect either inspected quality dimension.

- None.

### Unknowns

- The source does not establish that Shipment Larch and Shipment Birch failed for the same reason. It identifies Larch’s failure as finish acceptance and Birch’s as dimensional acceptance.
- The source does not establish that the packing-foam change solved or improved shipment quality, or what effect the foam had.
- The source does not establish why the supplier replaced the packing foam.
- The source does not establish the relationship between the foam replacement and the inspection results. It also reports that Birch used a different machining batch and that no isolated foam-effect test was done.
- The source does not establish how the inspected top-layer components compare with uninspected lower layers; no lower-layer counts are available, and the selected components were not a random sample.
- The recommended randomized deeper inspection has not been tested; the source does not report its results.

### Development sequence

- The source presents the inspection observations in this order: Larch (Facts 4–6), Birch (Facts 7–9), then Hazel (Fact 10). This is the order of presentation; the source does not establish that it is the shipments’ chronological order.
- The supplier replaced packing foam after Larch (Fact 13). The relationship between that replacement and the Birch or Hazel inspection results is UNKNOWN (Facts 14–15).
- The report recommends randomized deeper inspection before changing the acceptance rule (Fact 16); the recommendation has not been tested (Fact 17).

### Facts
1. The library describes the bilingual reading cards as “a visible expression of our commitment to language access”; this is the institution’s stated meaning, not a measured user response. (Source opening)
2. The pilot supplied 24 printed cards in Spanish and Arabic at two branches for four weeks. (Pilot description)
3. Staff received 18 unsolicited comments: 13 favorable and 5 reporting unclear wording. (Feedback)
4. Visitors were not sampled systematically, and the number who used the cards is unknown. (Feedback)
5. Staff found incorrect opening hours on three Arabic cards and revised them. (Card accuracy)
6. Spanish cards had correct opening hours, although two Spanish readers reported wording difficulty. (Card accuracy and feedback)
7. One Arabic reader praised the welcoming design while reporting a wrong closing time. (Feedback)
8. No comprehension or belonging survey was conducted. (Evaluation)
9. A website version is proposed, with no release date or completed implementation. (Next steps)
10. The annual circulation total predates the pilot and cannot measure its effect. (Evaluation)

### Interpretations
- The source documents favorable and critical unsolicited feedback, but does not establish that users’ comprehension or belonging improved. (Facts 3–4, 8)
- The source distinguishes factual accuracy from wording: reported wording difficulty occurred even where Spanish hours were correct, while Arabic cards had reported time errors. (Facts 5–7)

### Unknowns
- Whether comprehension or belonging improved is not established: there was no survey, users were not sampled systematically, and the number of card users is unknown. Favorable comments alone do not establish either outcome. (Facts 3–4, 8)
- The source does not establish how common the reported wording difficulties or time errors were among all users or cards beyond the specific reports and three revised Arabic cards. (Facts 3, 5–7)
- The pilot’s effect on circulation cannot be inferred from the annual total, which predates it. (Fact 10)
- Whether the proposed website version will be released, and when, is unknown. (Fact 9)

### Development sequence
- The library stated the cards’ intended meaning as a commitment to language access (Fact 1); it then supplied 24 cards at two branches for four weeks (Fact 2). Staff received unsolicited feedback during the pilot, including wording concerns and specific time errors; three Arabic cards were revised (Facts 3, 5–7). A website version is proposed but not implemented in the source’s account (Fact 9).

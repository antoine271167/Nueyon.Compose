### Facts
1. East recorded 14 pressure alerts in 20 operating days in April under the earlier valve schedule, and 8 in 20 days in May under a new schedule. (“During May”; “In April”)
2. East’s May industrial demand was 30% lower than April’s, and its alert threshold changed from 2.0 bar to 1.8 bar. (“May also had”)
3. The note does not report how many April alerts were between 1.8 and 2.0 bar. (“The note does not report”)
4. West retained the earlier valve schedule and 2.0 bar threshold in both months; its alerts fell from 12 to 7 as demand fell 28%. West is supplied by a separate pump, and the note calls it “contextual contrary evidence, not a randomized control.”
5. The operations team described the May East schedule as “a promising operational trial, not an established improvement.” No randomized assignment or threshold-adjusted reanalysis was performed. (“The operations team described”; “No randomized assignment”)
6. Maintenance costs and water quality were not measured. (“Maintenance costs and water quality were not measured.”)
7. A new remote sensor was planned for July and was not installed during either month. (“A new remote sensor is planned for July”)

### Interpretations
1. East’s observed alert count was lower in May than in April, but the comparison also involved changes in demand and the alert threshold (Facts 1–3).
2. West provides a contemporaneous decline under an unchanged schedule and threshold, but its separate pump and the stated lack of randomized control limit what the comparison establishes (Fact 4).

### Unknowns
- The source does not establish that the new East schedule caused or reduced pressure alerts; the reported count difference alone does not isolate its effect (Facts 1–5).
- The East comparison’s threshold effect cannot be assessed from the note because the count of April alerts between 1.8 and 2.0 bar is absent (Fact 3). No threshold-adjusted reanalysis was performed (Fact 5).
- The source does not establish how the schedule affected maintenance costs or water quality, which were not measured (Fact 6).

### Development sequence
- April: East recorded 14 alerts in 20 operating days under the earlier schedule; West recorded 12 while retaining its earlier schedule and 2.0 bar threshold (Facts 1, 4).
- May: East used the new schedule and recorded 8 alerts in 20 days; West recorded 7. Demand was lower at both reservoirs, and East’s threshold was lower (Facts 1, 2, 4).
- July: A remote sensor was planned, but was not installed during the April–May observations (Fact 7).

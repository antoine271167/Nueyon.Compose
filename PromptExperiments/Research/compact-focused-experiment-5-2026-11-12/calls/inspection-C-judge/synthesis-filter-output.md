### Facts

1. Larch met dimensional tolerance on all 40 inspected components, but 6 had surface scratches and it failed finish acceptance. (Source: “Shipment Larch”)
2. Birch had no scratches among 40 inspected components, but 4 were outside tolerance and it failed dimensional acceptance. (Source: “Shipment Birch”)
3. Hazel passed both dimensions on all 40 inspected components. (Source: “Shipment Hazel”)
4. The source states that passing one dimension does not imply passing the other. (Source: “Passing one dimension…”)
5. Inspectors selected accessible top-layer components, not a random shipment sample; no counts are available for uninspected lower layers. (Source: sampling limitations)
6. The supplier replaced packing foam after Larch, but Birch also used a different machining batch; no isolated foam-effect test was done. (Source: confounders)
7. The report recommends randomized deeper inspection before changing the acceptance rule. The recommendation has not been tested, and the report does not recommend dropping either dimension. (Source: recommendation)
8. A separate label-printing delay affected Hazel’s delivery timing, not either inspected quality dimension. (Source: Hazel delivery timing)

### Unknowns

- Larch and Birch did not fail for the same reason: Larch failed finish acceptance and Birch failed dimensional acceptance (Facts 1–2).
- The source does not establish that the foam change solved shipment quality. The foam effect was not isolated, and Birch also used a different machining batch (Fact 6).
- The accessible top-layer observations do not establish quality across each full shipment; lower-layer counts are unavailable (Fact 5).
- The proposed randomized deeper inspection has not been tested, so its results are unknown (Fact 7).

### Facts
1. City Memory is explicitly described as a fictional municipal archive, and the dossier as synthetic rather than a real archive report (opening statement).
2. The ten-week pilot, from 6 April to 14 June, created 900 catalogue records—300 each from Cedar, Elm and Rowan. The dossier defines digitization as a searchable catalogue entry plus a scanned page; paper originals remain in controlled storage (Sections 1–2).
3. The source treats chronological order, factual description and image publication as distinct dimensions: accurate names do not guarantee correct ordering, and legible images do not guarantee accurate metadata (Section 1, “These dimensions require separate treatment”).
4. In the reviewed records, all 60 Cedar entries were chronologically correct, but 9 had a mistranscribed vessel name. All 60 reviewed Elm vessel names matched the originals, but 8 entries were under the wrong year. Rowan passed both checks in all 60 reviewed records (Section 2).
5. Reviewers examined the first 20 items in three accessible boxes per collection, not a random sample of all 300 entries; fragile-binding boxes were excluded. The source says these observations are not full-collection error-rate estimates and that excluded material may differ in difficulty (Section 2).
6. On 8 July, staff corrected the nine Cedar vessel names and eight Elm dates. Reviewers rechecked those 17 corrected entries, not all 180 reviewed entries or the other 720 catalogue records; the source says this supports correction of the observed entries, not complete certification (Section 2).
7. Rights clearance is complete for 760 of the 900 scans. The other 140 are held internally: their catalogue descriptions are visible, but their page images are not publicly downloadable. Review of the remainder is forecast for next quarter, without a committed release date (Section 5).
8. The paper originals remain available by appointment subject to handling restrictions; the web catalogue supplements physical access rather than replacing every route to the collection (Section 6).
9. The committee approved designing a deeper random audit stratified by handwriting, print quality and collection. It has not approved a full-archive rollout or a claim that assisted cataloguing outperforms manual work; the audit has not been tested and has no new pass rate (Section 7).
10. The committee’s stated communication goal is to acknowledge that 900 catalogue entries exist and observed errors received specific corrections, while keeping distinct quality dimensions and the publication boundary visible (Section 7).

### Interpretations
- The pilot demonstrates a concrete catalogue output and specific corrections, but its checks do not establish that all records are accurate or that all images are publicly available (Facts 2, 4–7).
- The committee’s next step is further audit design, not approval of full rollout (Fact 9).

### Unknowns
- The source does not establish quality rates for all 900 records, all three full collections, or material excluded from review; the reviewed records were a non-random, accessible subset (Facts 4–5).
- It does not establish complete accuracy even for the reviewed records after correction: only the 17 corrected entries were rechecked, not all reviewed or remaining records (Fact 6).
- It does not establish that all 900 scans are publicly available: 140 are not downloadable pending rights review, and no release date is committed (Fact 7).
- It does not establish that the planned audit will be conducted, what it will find, or that a full-archive rollout will follow (Fact 9).

### Development sequence
- The pilot ran from 6 April to 14 June and produced 900 catalogue records (Fact 2).
- Review found different issues across collections: Cedar vessel-name errors, Elm year-placement errors, and no failures in the two checks among the 60 reviewed Rowan records (Fact 4).
- Staff corrected the nine Cedar names and eight Elm dates on 8 July; only those 17 entries were rechecked (Fact 6).
- The committee approved designing a stratified random audit, but not a full-archive rollout (Fact 9).

### Editorial relevance
- Core distinction between ordering, factual description and publication availability: Facts 3–8.
- Pilot output, observed errors, correction scope and sampling limits: Facts 2, 4–6; Unknowns 1–2.
- Limited next-step decision: Fact 9; Unknown 4.

### Facts

1. Webb’s operational configuration is approximately 70 ft long, 47 ft wide and 28 ft tall, too large for an available rocket; it was designed to fold into the Ariane 5 fairing, described as approximately 18 ft in diameter and 43 ft tall. (“Quick Facts”)
2. The source says Webb’s design had to meet the mission’s science requirements while also folding for launch and deploying in flight. (“Folding For Launch”)
3. Deployment involved over 50 major steps, 178 release mechanisms and over 300 possible single points of failure; the source says any one could have diminished or crippled the mission. It also states that repair was not possible at approximately one million miles from Earth. (“Quick Facts”; “In Depth”)
4. The solar array and high-gain antenna deployed automatically shortly after launch. The remaining deployments were choreographed from ground control by engineers and monitored through telemetry. (“Quick Facts”)
5. Webb separated from the Ariane 5 upper stage after about 26 minutes of thrust; deployments began minutes after separation. The launch was on Dec. 25, 2021. (“Unfolding Webb”; launch caption)
6. In the first week, the fore and aft sunshield pallets deployed; the spacecraft bus and telescope separated by extension of a roughly 2 m tower; membranes were unpinned, midbooms extended port side then starboard side, and the membranes were tensioned. Radiators were also released and deployed. (“In the first week”)
7. In the second week, the secondary-mirror tripod unfolded and latched, and both primary-mirror wings rotated and latched, completing the telescope’s fully deployed operational configuration. (“Second Week”)
8. During the first month, the telescope and instruments cooled for several weeks. Strategically placed heaters controlled cooling so components would shrink carefully and trapped water could escape as gas rather than freeze on mirrors or detectors. The team unlocked the primary mirror segments and secondary mirror and verified they could move; it also powered up instrument systems and made the final mid-course maneuver to the optimum orbit around L2. (“1st Month”)
9. In months two through four, the team checked out the optics and aligned the mirrors. Initial images showed distorted images of the same star because the primary segments were not yet aligned as one mirror; the source describes identifying segment-to-image correspondence and aligning the primary and secondary mirrors over the following months. (“In the second, third and fourth months”)
10. In months five and six, the team calibrated the instruments’ modes while observing representative targets and demonstrated tracking of moving solar-system targets. Early Release Observations were also made for release after commissioning. (“In the fifth and sixth months”)
11. The source says deployment proceeded with “remarkable smoothness”; Webb met thresholds for mirror alignment and later for instruments to come online and be calibrated, and final results exceeded requirements and expectations. These are the source’s reported conclusions. (“Quick Facts”)
12. After six months, Webb began its science mission and routine science operations. (“After six months”)
13. The source identifies design, development and intensive testing as keys to the deployment’s success. (“In Depth”)

### Unknowns

- The source does not give step-by-step telemetry results, identify any deployment anomaly or explain whether a particular issue was encountered and resolved. Its overall report of smooth deployment does not provide those details (Facts 3–4, 11).
- It does not quantify the alignment or instrument-calibration thresholds, provide detailed calibration results, or give an exact date for the start of routine science operations (Facts 8–12).
- The source characterizes design, development and testing as keys to success, but does not isolate their individual effects or provide comparative evidence for that attribution (Fact 13).

**Selected Story**
Webb’s route to routine science was longer than its deployment: folding it to fit inside the Ariane 5 fairing and then unfolding it in space were essential milestones, but cooling, mirror alignment and instrument calibration continued throughout a six-month commissioning period.

**Story Progression**
- **Context:** Webb had to launch folded because its operational configuration was too large for the available rocket. Its deployment required more than 50 major steps and involved over 300 possible single points of failure; repair was not possible at its distance from Earth.
- **Development:** By its second week, the secondary mirror and both primary-mirror wings had unfolded and latched, completing the telescope’s deployed configuration. That milestone did not mean it was ready for routine science: cooling took several weeks, mirror alignment followed in months two through four, and instrument-mode calibration in months five and six.
- **Endpoint:** After six months, Webb began routine science operations. Its deployment was a crucial achievement, but the remaining commissioning work shows why unfolding alone was not the finish line.

**Critical Constraints**
Do not imply deployment suffered setbacks or that commissioning was recovery from a failure. Do not claim specific calibration results beyond the reported transition to routine science operations.

Yellowstone’s wolves live and hunt in packs. Their social lives include cooperative care of offspring and group hunting—and their kills feed ravens, coyotes, bears, and invertebrates.

What wolves prey on has also changed. Between 1995–2000 and 2001–2007, the balance of elk calves and bull elk among early-winter prey reversed, a shift linked to changes in prey vulnerability and availability.

Wolf predation and its effects on elk are part of a wider picture that includes other predators, ungulate management outside the park, and weather. Yellowstone’s wolves are best understood through these relationships and changing conditions, not as a single force determining what happens across the ecosystem.

**Central Message**
Lowell’s reuse of its industrial heritage contributed to a broader revival, but did not shield the city from economic change: a 1992 account describes renewed activity and building reuse before a downturn by the late 1980s.

**Story Progression**
- **Context:** Textile employment fell from 17,000 in 1895 to 8,000 by 1936 as mills closed. One worker recalled being sent home repeatedly, unsure when work would be available.
- **Development:** In the 1960s, Lowell citizens proposed using the city’s architectural and cultural heritage in revitalization; political and business leaders backed the effort, and the city council endorsed it in 1972. Heritage and historical parks followed in 1974 and 1978. The account reports that more than 100 old buildings were rehabilitated for new uses in the late 1970s and early 1980s, alongside rising employment and business expansion. It also identifies the University of Lowell and Wang Laboratories as separate developments of the 1970s.
- **Endpoint:** By the late 1980s, the boom had ended amid a regional slowdown and difficulties in the computer industry, including Wang workforce cuts and restructuring. The revival was real in the account, but it was neither attributable to heritage efforts alone nor an escape from economic reversals.

**Critical Constraints**
Treat this as the perspective of a 1992 historical account, not a current measure of Lowell. Do not assign the recovery or building reuse solely to the parks or preservation effort; the account does not quantify the contributions of these or other factors. It does not establish what happened after 1992.

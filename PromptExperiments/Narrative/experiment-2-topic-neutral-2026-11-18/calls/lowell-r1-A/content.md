### Selected Story
Lowell’s shift from a declining textile city toward heritage-led redevelopment and new activity was a revival, but not an escape from economic change. The reuse of old industrial buildings formed part of a broader period of renewed activity; by the late 1980s, that boom had ended. The story should make this contrast clear through the perspective of a 1992 historical account, rather than present a simple recovery narrative.

- **Context:** Establish Lowell’s industrial decline through falling textile employment, mill closures, and the uncertainty experienced by workers.
- **Development:** Describe how citizens proposed using the city’s architectural and cultural heritage in a revitalization strategy, followed by heritage and historical parks. - **Endpoint:** Close with the late-1980s downturn, which ended the boom and underscores that heritage-led reuse was part of a broader revival, not a permanent escape from economic change.

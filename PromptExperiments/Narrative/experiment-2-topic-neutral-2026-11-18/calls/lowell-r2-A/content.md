### Selected Story
Lowell’s textile decline brought mill closures, falling employment and uncertainty for workers. From the 1960s, citizens proposed using the city’s architectural and cultural heritage as part of revitalization, an effort supported by political and business leaders. The later reuse of more than 100 old buildings accompanied renewed employment and business activity, but the revival was not a complete escape from economic change: by the late 1980s, a downturn had followed.

### Story Progression
- **Context:** Establish Lowell’s industrial decline through falling textile employment and mill closures; a worker’s recollection of repeatedly being sent home without knowing whether work would be available makes the disruption concrete.
- **Development:** Explain the citizen-led proposal to use Lowell’s architectural and cultural heritage in revitalization, followed by the city’s endorsement and the creation of heritage and historical parks. In the late 1970s and early 1980s, more than 100 old buildings were rehabilitated for new uses alongside rising employment and business expansion.

### Selected Story
Lowell’s shift from textile manufacturing toward heritage-led reuse took place against a long decline in textile employment and the uncertainty experienced by workers. In the 1960s, citizens proposed using the city’s architectural and cultural heritage as part of revitalization; political and business leaders supported the effort, and historic parks followed. More than 100 old buildings were later rehabilitated for new uses during a period of rising employment and business expansion. The central point is that this reuse formed part of a broader revival, not a lasting escape from economic change: a downturn had followed by the late 1980s.

### Story Progression
- **Context:** Establish Lowell’s textile decline through falling employment and the uncertainty it brought to workers, including one worker’s recollection of repeatedly being sent home without knowing whether work would be available.
- **Development:** Describe the citizen-led proposal to build on Lowell’s architectural and cultural heritage, its support from political and business leaders, and the later parks.

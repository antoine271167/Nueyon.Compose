**Selected Story**
Webb’s compact launch and carefully sequenced deployment brought it into its operational configuration, but unfolding was only an early milestone. Cooling, mirror alignment and instrument calibration continued through a six-month commissioning period before routine science began.

**Story Progression**
- **Context:** Webb had to fold into the Ariane 5 fairing because its operational configuration was too large for the available rocket. Deployment required more than 50 major steps, with over 300 possible single points of failure; repair at Webb’s distance from Earth was not possible.
- **Development:** By its second week, the secondary mirror and both primary-mirror wings had unfolded and latched. The telescope and instruments then cooled for several weeks; mirror alignment followed in months two through four, and instrument-mode calibration in months five and six.
- **Endpoint:** Webb began routine science operations after six months, showing that reaching its deployed configuration and becoming ready for science were distinct stages.

**Critical Constraints**
The reported deployment proceeded smoothly; do not imply that commissioning was recovery from a deployment failure. No specific calibration results are provided beyond the transition to routine science operations.

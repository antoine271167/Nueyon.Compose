**Selected Story**
Webb’s compact launch and successful deployment were only the beginning: reaching its fully deployed configuration in the second week did not yet make the telescope ready for routine science. Cooling, mirror alignment and instrument calibration continued through a six-month commissioning period.

**Story Progression**
- **Context:** Webb had to fold to fit inside the Ariane 5 fairing because its operational configuration was too large for the available rocket.
- **Development:** Deployment required more than 50 major steps and involved over 300 possible single points of failure; any one could have diminished or crippled the mission, and Webb was too far from Earth to repair. The process proceeded smoothly: by the second week, its secondary mirror and both primary-mirror wings were unfolded and latched.
- **Endpoint:** The telescope then cooled during the first month, underwent mirror alignment in months two through four, and instrument-mode calibration in months five and six. It began routine science operations after six months—a reminder that deployment completed a physical transformation, while commissioning established readiness for science.

**Critical Constraints:** Do not imply that deployment encountered anomalies or that commissioning was recovery from a failure. Do not claim specific calibration results beyond the transition to routine science operations.

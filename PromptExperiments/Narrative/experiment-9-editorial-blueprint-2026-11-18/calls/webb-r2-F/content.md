**Selected Story**
Webb’s compact launch and high-stakes deployment were only the beginning: reaching its fully deployed configuration in the second week did not mean it was ready for routine science. Cooling, mirror alignment and instrument calibration continued through a six-month commissioning period.

**Story Progression**
- **Context:** Webb had to fold to fit inside the Ariane 5 fairing. Its deployment required more than 50 major steps, with over 300 possible single points of failure; at Webb’s distance from Earth, repair was not possible.
- **Development:** The secondary mirror and both primary-mirror wings had unfolded and latched by the second week. The telescope and instruments then cooled for several weeks; mirror alignment took place in months two through four, followed by instrument-mode calibration in months five and six. Routine science operations began after six months.

**Evidence/Observed Results**
The completed deployment established Webb’s operational configuration, while the subsequent commissioning work marked the distinct path to routine science. The reported sequence was cooling, alignment, calibration, then routine operations.

**Editorial Conclusion**
Webb’s deployment was a major milestone, not the finish line: the telescope’s route to routine science depended on months of cooling and careful alignment and calibration after it had unfolded.

**Critical Constraints**
Deployment proceeded smoothly; do not invent anomalies or portray commissioning as recovery from a deployment failure. The available evidence gives no specific calibration results beyond the transition to routine science operations.

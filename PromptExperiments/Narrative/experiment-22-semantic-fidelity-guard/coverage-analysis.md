# Coverage

The curated invariant set has one relationship per source subject. A selected line is routing evidence that the checker reached a possible assertion. It does not demonstrate that surrounding claims were checked or that the selected text was interpreted correctly. The corpus has no independently enumerated census of every material claim in the historical briefs, so a brief-wide claim coverage percentage is unavailable. The scope of the measured coverage is the predeclared relationship per subject and the exact reference issue in each F2 brief. See metrics.json for exact target-in-candidate counts and category denominators.

The mechanical rule requires one recognized ratio on a line. EIA places two ratio clauses on the same line. Discovery finds the correct line, then the parser declines the ambiguous result. The independent fault probe reproduces the loss on a two-clause input. A one-clause EIA-style input blocks, but a one-clause editorial prohibition also blocks. The fingerprint needs both better scoping and positive assertion binding before another evaluation.

Wolf and Webb were reserved for development. Wolf demonstrates the utility of an explicitly grounded referent, although a human supplied the mapping. Webb shows that a sourced overlap can be present while onset is interpreted too permissively. The short NASA excerpt also depends on the relation annotation to explain what the chart measures. FAA shows the limit of a name with thin definition context, and Digital shows the difference between alternation between weeks and alternation between sprints. The challenge cohort includes causal reversal and an added adjustment purpose in one mutation. It contains no naturally generated causal overstatement.

Source-only annotation was manual. Scalable, independent invariant generation and automatic discovery of claims outside annotated relationships are untested. A production PASS would need a separately validated coverage policy.

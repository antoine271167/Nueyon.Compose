In the 1930s Franklin D. Roosevelt was interested in toll superhighways and possible employment benefits. The 1938 Federal-Aid Highway Act directed the Bureau of Public Roads chief to study a proposed six-route toll network. The 1944 Federal-Aid Highway Act authorized designating 40,000 miles for a national interstate network. The 1956 Act, dated June 29, authorized funding for highway construction and amended tax provisions for motor fuel, tires, trucks and buses. The National Archives identifies the 1956 law with establishing the interstate system.

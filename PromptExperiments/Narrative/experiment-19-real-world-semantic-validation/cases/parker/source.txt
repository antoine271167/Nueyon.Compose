The probe began its closest approach on December 24, 2024, coming within 3.8 million miles of the solar surface. It gathered observations in the corona around perihelion with several instruments including WISPR. WISPR images show the heliospheric current sheet and captured colliding coronal mass ejections at high resolution. Parker confirmed two types of slow solar wind. Scientists suspect non-Alfvénic slow wind could come from helmet streamers and Alfvénic slow wind from near coronal holes; NASA reports no final consensus on those origins.

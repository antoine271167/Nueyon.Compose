**Selected Story**
Webb’s path to routine science was longer than its deployment. Its compact launch design made it possible to fit inside the Ariane 5 fairing, but unfolding into its operational configuration was only the first milestone; cooling, mirror alignment and instrument calibration continued through a six-month commissioning period.

**Story Progression**
- **Context:** Webb had to fold for launch because its operational configuration was too large for the available rocket. Deployment involved more than 50 major steps and over 300 possible single points of failure, with no possibility of repair at Webb’s distance from Earth.
- **Development:** By its second week, the secondary mirror and both primary-mirror wings had unfolded and latched, completing the telescope’s fully deployed configuration. The work of commissioning continued: cooling occupied the first month, mirror alignment took place in months two through four, and instrument-mode calibration followed in months five and six.

**Evidence/Observed Results**
Webb began routine science operations after six months. The deployment process proceeded smoothly; the reported risks describe what could have happened, not failures that did happen.

**Editorial Conclusion**
Webb’s deployment was a major engineering achievement, but not the same as readiness for routine science. The distinction is clear in the months of cooling, alignment and calibration that followed.

**Critical Constraints**
Do not portray commissioning as recovery from a deployment failure or invent specific anomalies. Do not claim particular calibration results beyond the reported transition to routine science operations.

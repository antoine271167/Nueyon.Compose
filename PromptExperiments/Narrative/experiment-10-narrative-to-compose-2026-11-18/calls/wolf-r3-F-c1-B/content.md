Yellowstone’s wolves are hunters, but their place in the ecosystem is about more than hunting.

A wolf pack is a social family. Members hunt together and cooperate in caring for young. And wolf kills feed other animals, including ravens, coyotes, bears and invertebrates.

What wolves prey on can change, too. Between 1995–2000 and 2001–2007, the relative shares of elk calves and bull elk in their early-winter prey reversed. Prey vulnerability and availability are part of that picture; wolf predation and its effects on elk also occur amid other predators, ungulate management outside the park and weather.

Yellowstone’s wolves are part of a living network of relationships. Their changing impacts cannot be reduced to one predator directing the ecosystem.

### Selected Story
Lowell’s shift from textile manufacturing toward heritage-based reuse formed part of a wider revival, not a complete escape from economic change. After textile employment fell and mill work became uncertain for workers, citizens promoted using the city’s architectural and cultural heritage in revitalization. More than 100 old buildings were later rehabilitated for new uses amid rising employment and business expansion; by the late 1980s, the boom had ended. The reader should understand this as a layered change, viewed through a 1992 historical account—not a simple recovery story or a current measure of the city.

### Selected Story
Lowell’s shift from textile decline toward heritage-led redevelopment brought renewed activity and new uses for its industrial buildings, but it did not mark a lasting escape from economic change. The story follows the contrast between falling textile employment, citizens’ efforts to make the city’s architectural and cultural heritage part of revitalization, and the reuse of more than 100 old buildings alongside rising employment and business expansion. The later downturn by the late 1980s makes clear that this revival was part of a broader, uneven history—not a simple recovery story.

### Story Progression
- **Context:** Establish Lowell’s industrial decline through falling textile employment and the uncertainty workers experienced as mills closed.
- **Development:** Describe citizens’ heritage-based revitalization effort, its support from political and business leaders, and the subsequent heritage and historical parks. Show the shift through the reported reuse of more than 100 buildings alongside rising employment and business expansion, without assigning that activity to the parks alone.
- **Endpoint:** Bring the story to the late-1980s downturn amid a regional slowdown and difficulties in the computer industry. The contrast shows that heritage reuse accompanied a broader revival, but did not end Lowell’s exposure to economic change.

### Critical Constraints
- Treat this as the perspective of a 1992 historical account, not a measurement of Lowell today.
- Do not present the parks or preservation effort as the sole cause of renewed activity; the account also identifies other developments, including the University of Lowell and Wang Laboratories.
- Do not characterize the revival as a complete or lasting recovery.

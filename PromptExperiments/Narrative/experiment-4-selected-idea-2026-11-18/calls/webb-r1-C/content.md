**Selected Story** Webb’s compact launch design made it possible to fit the telescope inside an Ariane 5 rocket, but unfolding it was only an early milestone. The telescope reached its fully deployed configuration in its second week; cooling, mirror alignment and instrument calibration continued through the six-month commissioning period before routine science operations began.

**Story Progression**
- **Context:** Webb had to fold for launch because its operational configuration was too large for the available rocket. Deployment involved more than 50 major steps and over 300 possible single points of failure, with no possibility of repair at Webb’s distance from Earth.
- **Development:** By the second week, the secondary mirror and both primary-mirror wings had unfolded and latched. The telescope and instruments then cooled for several weeks; mirror alignment followed in months two through four, and instrument-mode calibration in months five and six.
- **Endpoint:** Webb began routine science operations after six months. Reaching its deployed shape was a major threshold, but not the same as being ready for routine science.

**Critical Constraints:** Deployment proceeded smoothly; do not invent anomalies or portray commissioning as recovery from a deployment failure. The source does not specify calibration results beyond the transition to routine science operations.

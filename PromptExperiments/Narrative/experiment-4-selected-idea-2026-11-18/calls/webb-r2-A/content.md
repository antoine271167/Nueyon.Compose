### Selected Story
Webb’s deployment was a major milestone, but it was not the same as being ready for routine science. Designed to fold inside the Ariane 5 fairing, the telescope reached its fully deployed configuration in its second week; cooling, mirror alignment and instrument calibration continued over the six-month commissioning period. The reader should understand that unfolding marked one stage in Webb’s journey to routine science, not its completion.

### Story Progression
- **Context:** Webb had to be folded for launch because its operational configuration was too large for the available rocket. Deployment involved many steps, with potential failure points and no possibility of repair at Webb’s distance from Earth.
- **Development:** By its second week, the secondary mirror and both primary-mirror wings had unfolded and latched, completing the telescope’s deployed configuration. That milestone was followed by weeks of cooling, months of mirror alignment and later instrument-mode calibration.
- **Endpoint:** Webb began routine science operations after six months of commissioning, distinguishing completion of deployment from readiness for routine science.

### Critical Constraints
- Present deployment risks as stakes, not as evidence that something went wrong; deployment proceeded smoothly.
- Do not portray commissioning as recovery from a deployment failure or claim calibration results beyond the transition to routine science operations.

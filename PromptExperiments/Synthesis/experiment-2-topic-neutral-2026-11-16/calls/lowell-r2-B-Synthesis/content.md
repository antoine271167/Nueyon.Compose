### Central insight
Lowell’s revival combined a collaborative effort to reuse its industrial heritage with other economic developments; although employment rose and old buildings found new uses in the late 1970s and early 1980s, the boom had ended by the late 1980s.

### Evidence
1. By 1936, textile employment had fallen to 8,000, and the source describes mills as empty, reduced, or demolished. Workers’ recollections also record hardship and uncertainty as work disappeared. (Research Facts 3–4)
2. Lowell citizens developed a revitalization strategy centered on the city’s architectural and cultural heritage. Political and business leaders supported the effort, and it led to state and national historic parks and a preservation commission. (Research Facts 7–8)
3. In the late 1970s and early 1980s, employment rose, businesses expanded, and more than 100 old buildings were rehabilitated for new uses. The account also identifies the University of Lowell and Wang Laboratories among other developments of the 1970s. (Research Facts 9–10)
4. By the late 1980s, the source says the boom had ended as the regional and computer economies cooled; companies closed or relocated, and Wang cut most of its workforce and restructured. (Research Fact 11)

### What the narrative should emphasize
Keep the focus on the contrast between a collaborative heritage-reuse effort and the limits of the subsequent revival. The building reuse and employment rise make the change concrete; the late-1980s downturn prevents it from becoming a simple recovery story. Describe the documented actors’ direction and the 1992 account, rather than prescribing an approach for readers or presenting heritage preservation as the proven cause of recovery.

### What should be de-emphasized
The detailed chronology of mill closures, the full wartime labor story, and descriptions of visitor activities, public art, and other contemporary city features are not needed to establish this story. The University of Lowell and Wang can be mentioned briefly as other developments, but do not make either a separate success story.

### Gaps and uncertainty
The source does not quantify the separate contributions of the parks, preservation programs, university, Wang, or other factors to employment or recovery. It also does not establish who benefited from the revival or whether it offset earlier worker losses. Treat the account as a historical perspective published in 1992; it does not establish what happened after that or describe Lowell’s current conditions.

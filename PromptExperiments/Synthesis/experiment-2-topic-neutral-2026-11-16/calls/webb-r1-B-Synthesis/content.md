### Central insight
Webb’s unfolding completed its physical deployment in the second week, but that was not the same as being ready for routine science: cooling, mirror alignment and instrument calibration continued over the following months, and routine science operations began after six months.

### Evidence
1. Webb was designed to fold into the Ariane 5 fairing because its operational configuration was too large for the available rocket.
2. Deployment involved more than 50 major steps and over 300 possible single points of failure; the source says repair would not be possible at Webb’s distance from Earth. The remaining deployments were choreographed from ground control and monitored through telemetry.
3. The secondary mirror and primary-mirror wings latched into place in the second week, completing Webb’s fully deployed operational configuration.
4. Over the following months, the telescope and instruments cooled, the mirrors were aligned, and instrument modes were calibrated. Webb began routine science operations after six months.

### What the narrative should emphasize
Center the story on the distinction between physical deployment and readiness for routine science, using Evidence 3 and 4. Evidence 1 and 2 can briefly establish why folding and deployment mattered, but need not become a detailed account of every mechanism or deployment step. Explain the documented sequence; do not turn it into advice for readers or a claim that this approach is universally superior.

### What should be de-emphasized
The detailed sequence of sunshield, mirror, and other component deployments is secondary unless one example is needed to clarify the physical deployment milestone. Detailed mid-course maneuvers, target observations, and early-release images are not needed to establish the central distinction.

### Gaps and uncertainty
The source reports that deployment proceeded smoothly but does not identify specific anomalies or issues encountered and resolved. Do not imply that a particular failure was narrowly avoided, or that the documented risks caused the months of cooling, alignment, or calibration.

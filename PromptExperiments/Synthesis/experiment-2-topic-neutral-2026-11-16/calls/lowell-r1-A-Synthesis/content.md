### Central insight
The 1992 account portrays Lowell’s revival as a civic effort to reuse its industrial heritage amid continuing economic change—not as a linear recovery: historic preservation and building reuse accompanied renewed activity, but the late-1980s boom ended.

### Evidence
1. By 1936, textile employment had fallen to 8,000, and only three of Lowell’s first large mills remained. Workers’ recollections in the account describe job insecurity and hardship during the decline.
2. In the 1960s, Lowell citizens developed a revitalization strategy centered on the city’s architectural and cultural heritage. Political and business leaders and government agencies cooperated; the effort included heritage parks and preservation institutions.
3. The account reports rising employment and business expansion in the late 1970s and early 1980s, alongside more than 100 old buildings rehabilitated for new uses. It does not attribute those developments solely to the parks.
4. By the late 1980s, the account says the boom had ended amid a cooling regional economy and tighter computer industry; Wang cut most of its workforce and restructured.

### What the narrative should emphasize
Keep the story on the contrast between the civic heritage-reuse effort and the later end of the boom. Use the worker recollections to make the earlier industrial decline human, and the reused buildings as a concrete example of the revival. Describe these developments in Lowell’s history; do not turn them into advice for other cities or claim that preservation caused the recovery.

### What should be de-emphasized
Do not expand into a full chronology of textile labor, every park or preservation milestone, or a broader account of all 1970s economic developments. The University of Lowell and Wang’s arrival are relevant background, but are not needed to establish this story; avoid presenting any one contribution as the cause of recovery.

### Gaps and uncertainty
The account does not quantify the separate contributions of parks, preservation, the university, Wang, or other factors to recovery, nor establish whether the revival offset losses for affected workers. It is a 1992 account and provides no basis for claims about Lowell’s conditions or the durability of the revival after that period.

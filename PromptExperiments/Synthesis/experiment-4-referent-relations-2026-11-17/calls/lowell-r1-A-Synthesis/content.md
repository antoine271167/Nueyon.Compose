### Central insight
Lowell’s heritage-led reuse of its industrial past was part of a broader revival, not a complete escape from economic change: the 1992 account describes renewed activity and building reuse, followed by a downturn by the late 1980s.

### Evidence
1. Textile employment fell from 17,000 in 1895 to 8,000 by 1936, amid mill closures and departures. A worker recalled being sent home repeatedly without knowing whether work would be available.
2. In the 1960s, Lowell citizens proposed using the city’s architectural and cultural heritage in a revitalization strategy. Political and business leaders supported the effort, and the city council endorsed the idea in 1972; heritage and historical parks followed in 1974 and 1978.
3. The source reports that, in the late 1970s and early 1980s, more than 100 old buildings were rehabilitated for new uses alongside rising employment and business expansion. It does not attribute these developments solely to the parks.
4. The account also identifies the University of Lowell and Wang Laboratories as separate developments of the 1970s. By the late 1980s, the boom had ended amid a regional slowdown and difficulties in the computer industry, including Wang’s workforce cuts and restructuring.

### What the narrative should emphasize
Keep the story on the contrast between industrial decline, heritage-based redevelopment, and the later downturn. The citizen-led preservation effort and the reported reuse of buildings provide the clearest evidence of the shift; the other 1970s developments and late-1980s reversal help prevent a simple, parks-caused recovery narrative. Treat this as the perspective of a 1992 historical account, and describe the actors’ documented direction rather than prescribing an approach for readers.

### What should be de-emphasized
The detailed history of mill owners’ decisions, the full sequence of textile closures, and descriptions of every park, preservation program, or later city activity are unnecessary unless a specific detail serves the central contrast. Avoid presenting the University or Wang as sole causes of recovery.

### Gaps and uncertainty
The source does not quantify the separate contributions of the parks, preservation programs, university, Wang, or other factors to recovery. It also does not establish whether the revival endured after the late-1980s downturn or describe conditions after its 1992 account.

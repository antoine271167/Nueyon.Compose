### Central insight
Webb’s deployment into its full configuration was not the same as being ready for routine science: after the telescope was fully deployed in its second week, months of cooling, mirror alignment and instrument calibration followed, and routine science began after six months.

### Evidence
1. Webb was too large in its operational configuration for the available rocket fairing, so it was designed to fold for launch and deploy in flight.
2. Deployment involved more than 50 major steps, 178 release mechanisms and over 300 possible single points of failure; the source says a failure could have diminished or crippled the mission, with no possibility of repair at Webb’s distance from Earth.
3. The telescope reached its fully deployed configuration in the second week. Cooling, mirror alignment and instrument calibration continued over the following months; Webb began routine science operations after six months.

### What the narrative should emphasize
Center the story on the distinction between physical deployment and operational readiness, using the second-week deployment and the six-month point as the key markers. The folding requirement and deployment risks can establish what made the initial unfolding consequential. Explain Webb’s own commissioning process; do not turn it into advice for readers or a claim that this approach is universally superior.

### What should be de-emphasized
Detailed dimensions, individual deployment mechanisms, every commissioning task and technical calibration specifics are unnecessary unless one detail helps clarify the central contrast. Avoid turning the story into a step-by-step account of the entire deployment.

### Gaps and uncertainty
The source reports that deployment proceeded smoothly but does not identify specific anomalies or explain whether any issue was encountered and resolved. Do not invent a crisis or a recovery narrative.

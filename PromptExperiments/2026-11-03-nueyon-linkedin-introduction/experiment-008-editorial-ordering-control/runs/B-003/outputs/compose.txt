What does orchestration look like in practice? Nuëyon.Compose offers a concrete answer: a six‑stage, responsibility‑driven workflow that maps distinct tasks to separate stages rather than relying on a single general‑purpose chat.

The workflow breaks content work into six stages: Idea (set the subject), Discover & Select (narrow the direction), Research (gather source material), Synthesize (organize and structure findings), Narrative (establish the central story), and Compose (produce the finished content).

Each stage has a specific responsibility, so the work of defining a subject, gathering sources, shaping the argument, and producing final copy are treated as separate steps in a coordinated process.

That structure is presented as a concrete expression of orchestration — the principle of assigning distinct responsibilities to specialized agents coordinated by an orchestration layer — not as evidence of measured outcomes or a validated implementation.

Seen this way, Compose is a design model: it shows one way to turn the abstract idea of orchestration into a staged workflow for content work.

The central point is straightforward: orchestration here means breaking a complex task into responsibility‑driven stages and arranging them in a clear sequence. Compose demonstrates that design approach rather than claiming proven results.

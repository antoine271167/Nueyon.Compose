Selected Story
Nuëyon is being presented as an AI-first company and Nuëyon.Compose is the first concrete product direction that makes that identity tangible: a deliberately narrow, AI-native orchestration workflow intended to turn ideas and accumulated knowledge into useful, well-structured content. The article should position Compose as an initial, design-first expression of Nuëyon’s ambition—not as proof of commercial launch or market traction—and make clear that this staged, orchestration-centered approach is the company’s starting point for product development.

Story Progression
- Context: Briefly introduce Nuëyon’s aim to operate as an AI-first software company building tools and knowledge for other AI-first builders, and state that Compose is presented as the company’s first concrete product direction.
- Development: Explain, at a high level, what Compose is: an AI-native, staged content workflow (one concrete example: Idea → Discover & Select → Research → Synthesize → Narrative → Compose) implemented as an orchestration approach that coordinates specialized stages/agents; note the current emphasis is deliberately narrow—establishing this core workflow first. Keep implementation details subordinate to the orchestration concept.
- Endpoint: Close by reiterating the central insight: Compose defines Nuëyon’s initial product direction as an orchestration-focused, staged workflow for turning ideas and accumulated knowledge into structured content, and roadmap items should be presented as directional priorities rather than committed launches.

Critical Constraints
- Do not attribute decisions or actions to an unspecified team, organization, or collective; describe product design directly and avoid inventing actors.
- Do not state or imply that Compose is launched, commercially available, or has paying customers or market traction.
- Do not claim demonstrated productivity gains, cost savings, superior performance, or validated outcomes for Compose unless supported by evidence.
- Do not present roadmap items, timelines, release dates, business model, or distribution strategy as finalized commitments—treat them as directional priorities.
- Do not invent motivations, causes, or benefits (for example, do not assert why the design changed or that it produced specific improvements).
- Do not imply the product operates entirely without human involvement or that an “AI-only” ambition is precisely defined or implemented.
